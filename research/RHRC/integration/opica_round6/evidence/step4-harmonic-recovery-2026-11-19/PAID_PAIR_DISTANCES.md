# Which pair distances can be paid without a correlation estimate

**DERIVED.** This note applies to both RATIONAL_SIGNED_COVARIANCE.md and DYADIC_TREE.md. Let \(s=\lceil\sqrt{\lceil\log_2 X\rceil}\rceil,\ H=2^s=X^{o(1)}\). All pairs with \(0<r-q\le H\) have total absolute weight \(O(H\log(2X))=X^{o(1)}\), even before removing large common divisors. In a shell \([U,2U)\) the rational unordered-pair weight is at most \(4/U\); the sum of dyadic-tree weights for any one unordered pair is also at most \((2/U)\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k}=4/U\). There are at most \(UH\) such pairs per shell, and \(O(\log X)\) shells.

For the tree there is a second absolute payment. From a base interval of length \(H_0\), level \(k\) has nodes of length \(H_0/2^k\). In an entire shell, all ordered-pair absolute weights at that level cost at most \(H_0/4^k\), with the same inequality after clipping endpoints or imposing the GCD cutoff. The sum over \(2k\ge\log_2 H_0\) is \(O(1)\) per shell. Thus the search can remove both the fine levels and every pair at the stated short additive distances from the exact signed covariance. Their union costs \(X^{o(1)}\). The remaining important pairs have large additive distance, close relative location, and small common divisor. The proof needs their aggregate signed contribution.

Available pointwise or almost-all short-interval cancellation gives some control on unweighted Möbius sums, and the companion sign-matching calculation pays most local labels. For example, a bound \(O(H_0/\log^A U)\) on each long base-cell sum, even if valid at every cell at fixed \(A\), would give only \(O(H_0/\log^{2A}U)\) for its shell's base-level energy after summation. Here \(H_0=U^{1-o(1)}\). This calculation explains the specific size gap for that application. A bound for every **fixed** additive gap likewise concerns pairs in a sector already paid at subpower cost.
