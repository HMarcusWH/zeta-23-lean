## The arithmetic still required

At order \(n\), \(z_{n,r}\) is the signed contribution of rough squarefree divisor \(r\) after integrating its actual time history against the same localized Laguerre test as every other divisor. For an ordered pair \(r,l\), cancel its common divisor and put \(m=rl/\gcd(r,l)^2\). Sum \(z_{n,r}\overline{z_{n,l}}\) over just the pairs with
\[
 \log r,\log l\le 2.33\cdot10^{-25}n+2,\qquad
 m>\exp(n/\log(n+2)),\qquad P^+(m)>n^2\log(n+2).
\]
Here \(P^+(m)\) is the largest prime factor left after cancellation. Call the sum \(S_n\). **It would suffice to prove directly from the arithmetic that for every \(\epsilon>0\) there are arbitrarily large orders \(n\) with \(S_n\le e^{\epsilon n}\).** Every omitted edge has been accounted for in the proof: the aggregate absolute contribution of the two lower ranges is subexponential, and the aggregate absolute upper-endpoint error tends to zero. A rightward zero would instead make \(S_n\) exponentially positive at every sufficiently large order. Neither the individual prime signs nor the positive diagonal give the needed upper. The bounds are asymptotic; at practical orders the very small upper cutoff can make the remaining range empty. Definition, proof and exact finite graph test
