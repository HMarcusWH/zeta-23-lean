# Why finite local Taylor evidence and positive Euler factors cannot propagate the bound

**19 November 2026. Exact countermodel for an inference, using the same OPICA pulse.** Every finite collection of the actual [single-scalar prime sums](SINGLE_SCALAR_CAYLEY.md) can be approximated arbitrarily closely by an Euler product which agrees at arbitrarily many initial primes, has nonnegative Dirichlet coefficients and nonnegative logarithmic-derivative coefficients at every prime power, and has an explicitly inserted rightward zero. The modified coefficients do not satisfy the original identity \(\Lambda=\mu*\log\). This test identifies which arithmetic information a proof must go beyond; it is not a counterexample for zeta.

Fix \(\beta=3/5\), any \(\gamma>3\cdot10^{12}\), and an integer cutoff \(N\ge6\). Put \(a=1-\beta=2/5\), \(\zeta_{>N}(u)=\zeta(u)\prod_{p\le N}(1-p^{-u})\), and
\[
 Z_N(s)=\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta_{>N}(s+a-i\gamma)\zeta_{>N}(s+a+i\gamma)}.
\]
Every factor at \(p\le N\) is the actual zeta factor. For \(p>N\) its factor is \((1-c_px)(1-\overline c_p x)/(1-x)\), with \(x=p^{-s}\) and \(c_p=p^{-a+i\gamma}\). Its coefficient of \(x\) is \(1-2\Re c_p>0\), since \(2p^{-2/5}<1\) for \(p\ge7\). Every coefficient of \(x^k\), \(k\ge2\), is \(|1-c_p|^2>0\). Meanwhile the coefficients of \(-Z_N'/Z_N\) are supported on the very same prime powers and equal
\[
  \Lambda(p^k)\left[1-2\,\mathbf1_{p>N}\,p^{-ka}\cos(k\gamma\log p)\right]>0.
\]
For any finite set of orders in (2) of the scalar note, their differences from the actual coefficients tend to zero as \(N\to\infty\). Indeed the absolute sum is bounded by the deleted tails of \(C_{j,n}\sum_{m>N}(1+\log m)^{n+1}m^{-3/2-a}\). The same conclusion holds for any finite set of ordinary Euler Taylor derivatives in a fixed compact part of \(\Re s>1\); simply increase the log power and keep a positive margin in the abscissa. The perturbed ordinary moment norms on \(\Re s=3/2\) also converge at each fixed order: the difference field tends pointwise to zero, its magnitude is bounded by \(C_j(1+t)e^{t/2}\), and dominated convergence applies to the gamma norm of the difference.

At \(s=\beta+i\gamma\) one denominator factor has the genuine pole of zeta at 1; its finite factors are nonzero there. The other shifted factor is finite and nonzero at \(1+2i\gamma\). The numerator is finite (a possible zero adds multiplicity). Thus both \(\beta\pm i\gamma\) are zeros of \(Z_N\). For the *difference* of the two logarithmic derivatives these give simple poles with nonzero residues. All other poles of that difference coming from nontrivial zeros of the shifted copies are to the left of \(\Re s=1/2\), since the externally verified ceiling gives \(\Re\rho<0.874\) and \(0.874-a<1/2\). The finitely many correction factors introduce poles only on \(\Re s=-a\); trivial zeros are further left. The native source transform is nonzero at both inserted shifted positions. For each small real \(\tau\), the two inserted poles map to a conjugate pair at \(\tau=0\) and split in modulus for \(\tau>0\). Choose a fixed disk with radius strictly between their common limiting modulus and 1. Subtract their two principal parts in the Cayley variable.
