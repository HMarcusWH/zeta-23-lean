# Unshifted signed covariance and recurrence of the actual critical modes

**19 November 2026. Paper result.** With one slowly varying modulation, the actual critical-line contribution to the Poisson-truncated prime scalar returns arbitrarily close to zero on sets of positive lower density. The diagonal of its projected arithmetic graph has a strictly positive limit. If every nontrivial zero is on the critical line, their combination gives negative **unshifted** off-diagonal covariance at positive lower density. If any rightward zero exists, that same covariance is positive at every sufficiently late order. The recurrence for the entire prime field remains an independent arithmetic obligation.

Use the same native prime-power field \(P\), integer-drive Möbius shifts \(c_r\) with \(P=\sum_r c_r\), and fixed \(Y=10^7\) as before. Use the localization's prescribed taper \(q_n(t)=k^{-1}\sum_{l=m+1}^{m+k}Q_l(\lambda t)\) with \(\lambda=10^6\), \(m=\lceil\alpha n\rceil\), \(\alpha=2.27\cdot10^{-19}\), \(k=\lceil m/100\rceil\). Take the *single* test \(f_n(t)=\sqrt2e^{-t}L_n(2t)e^{-i\tau_n t}q_n(t)\) with **\(\tau_n=1/\log(n+3)\)**. For the bilinear integral \(T_n(g)=\int f_n(t)g(t)dt\), form the exact arithmetic difference
\[
 B_n=T_n(P),\qquad D_n=\sum_r|T_n(c_r)|^2,\qquad
 R_n=|B_n|^2-D_n.
\]
This is precisely the ordered, signed contribution of distinct rough squarefree labels. At fixed \(n\), all sums and their products in the projected identity converge absolutely: the integrand used to sum the absolute shifts is bounded by a constant times \(e^{t/2}(1+t)\), while the fixed-order Laguerre probe and its taper decay exponentially. [Exact rough labels](../../step4-correlation-2026-11-19/FIXED_SIEVE_SIGN.md) · [Poisson comparison](../POISSON_PRIME_RANGE.md) · [Stable-mode estimates](POISSON_STABLE_MODES.md)

Write \(p=\rho-1/2\), \(a_p=-m_\rho J(p)\), and \(r_p(\tau)=(-1-p+i\tau)/(1-p+i\tau)\). Combining multiplicities, \(\sum_p|a_p|<\infty\). For a critical ordinate \(\gamma\), the retained scalar contribution equals
\[
 \frac{\sqrt2 a_{i\gamma}}{1-i\gamma+i\tau_n}
       w\!\left(\frac{2\lambda}{1+(\gamma-\tau_n)^2}\right)r_{i\gamma}(\tau_n)^n+o_\gamma(1),
\]
where \(w(v)\) is 1 on \(v\le\alpha\), decreases linearly to 0 on \([\alpha,1.01\alpha]\), and is 0 afterwards. To check this, the Taylor coefficients in the Poisson variable concentrate around order \(nv\) in absolute-sum norm: the part \(|j-nv|>\epsilon n\) costs \(O_\epsilon(n^{-1/4})\) by weighted Parseval and \(\sum_{|j-nv|>\epsilon n}|j-nv|^{-2}=O_\epsilon(1/n)\). Inside this range the averaging weight differs from \(w(v)\) by \(O(\epsilon)+o(1)\), its absolute coefficient sum staying bounded on each compact ordinate set. Then let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). Fejér gives the separate uniform bound \(201\sqrt2\) per unit residue, allowing the error to be summed over all actual critical zeros. The initial and trivial \(L^2\) correction contributes \(o(1)\). The genuine same-test diagonal has the unconditional limit
\[
 D_*=\frac{c_Y}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{|\widehat V_Y(\xi)|^2}{1+\xi^2}
             w(2\lambda/(1+\xi^2))^2\,d\xi>0 . \tag{1}
\]
Indeed the fixed-sieve discrepancy for harmonic sampling has a tail primitive tending to zero, and convolving \(f_n\) against the reflected \(V_Y\) gives uniform \(H^1\) bounds. On bounded log intervals the resulting values vanish by weak convergence with the compact initial contribution; the negative half-line convolution is Hilbert–Schmidt since \(\int_0^\infty u|V_Y(u)|^2du<\infty\). Thus the harmonic diagonal approaches the continuous convolution square integral times \(c_Y\). Its limit (1) follows by Plancherel and the preceding pointwise multiplier at real frequencies, dominated using the uniform Fejér bound. \(V_Y\) is nonzero with superexponentially decaying tail; its transform is entire, hence cannot vanish throughout the retained high-frequency interval. No numerical estimate of the size of \(D_*\) is needed.

## Why the critical phases return without an independence conjecture

For any finite collection of **distinct** real critical ordinates \(\gamma_j\), including opposite signs, the vectors \((r_{i\gamma_j}(\tau_n)^n)_j\) are equidistributed in their entire product of circles. This uses deterministic modulation. It makes no assertion that the ordinates themselves are linearly independent.

Choose the real analytic argument \(\theta_\gamma(u)=\pi+2\arctan(\gamma-u)\) on a neighborhood of zero. For any nonzero integer vector \(m\), \(h(u)=\sum_j m_j\theta_{\gamma_j}(u)\) is nonconstant. Its derivative is the rational function \(-2\sum_j m_j/[1+(\gamma_j-u)^2]\), whose poles at the distinct \(\gamma_j\pm i\) cannot cancel. Thus \(h(u)=h(0)+cu^l+O(u^{l+1})\) for some finite \(l\ge1\), \(c\ne0\). Put \(F(x)=x h(1/\log(x+3))\). Differentiating the convergent local series gives
\[
 F'(x)-h(0)\sim c/(\log x)^l,\qquad F''(x)\sim-lc/[x(\log x)^{l+1}] .
\]
The averages of \(e^{iF(n)}\) tend to zero. If \(e^{ih(0)}\ne1\), the successive unit-circle ratios tend to that constant; shifting the sum by one gives the assertion. Otherwise subtract the integer multiple of \(2\pi n\). The remaining successive phase increments are eventually monotone, of one sign, with size comparable to \((\log N)^{-l}\) on \([N,2N]\). The identity \(e^{iF(n)}=[e^{iF(n+1)}-e^{iF(n)}]/[e^{i(F(n+1)-F(n))}-1]\) and summation by parts bound this interval sum by \(O((\log N)^l)\). The reciprocal denominator is \(-1/2-(i/2)\cot((F(n+1)-F(n))/2)\), whose monotone imaginary part has the same total variation bound. Dividing by the number of terms and taking dyadic blocks proves the assertion. Vanishing of every nontrivial Fourier character gives the stated equidistribution by approximation with trigonometric polynomials.

For every \(\epsilon>0\), select finitely many conjugate pairs with remaining actual absolute residue weights below a sufficiently small multiple of \(\epsilon\). The two limiting amplitudes at \(\pm\gamma\) are conjugates and have the same modulus because \(j\) is real and even, \(m_{\bar\rho}=m_\rho\), and \(w\) is even as a function of the ordinate. Select independent target phases making the members of each pair cancel exactly. Small neighborhoods of those targets still have total magnitude below \(\epsilon/2\) and positive Haar measure. Equidistribution and the absolute tail show that the **actual critical component** is below \(\epsilon\) in modulus on a set of positive lower density. This claim about the critical component holds unconditionally, even if the zeta function also has rightward zeros.

If \(d=0\), this already describes the complete \(B_n\) up to \(o(1)\). Taking \(\epsilon<\sqrt{D_*}/2\) proves \(R_n<0\) at positive lower density. If \(d>0\), the choice \(n\tau_n^2\to\infty\) selects one exponentially dominant visible pole exactly as in the [single-scalar proof](SINGLE_SCALAR_CAYLEY.md). The highpass error at high ordinates is exponentially small, and the remaining stable contribution is bounded. Consequently \(|B_n|\to\infty\) exponentially at every late order, while \(D_n\to D_*\). We obtain the equivalences
\[
 d=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \{n:R_n<0\}\text{ has positive lower density}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R_n\le0\text{ at arbitrarily late orders} . \tag{2}
\]
The missing step is to establish the cofinal sign directly from the arithmetic, without assuming \(d=0\). Equidistribution gives returns of the stable component but cannot cancel an exponentially dominating rightward mode. The stated results use the slower prescribed modulation; the earlier cube-root numerical samples concern a different sequence.

## A two-polynomial alternative with smaller modulation

Use \(\tau_n=\log(n+2)/(n+1)\), and replace \(L_n\) in the test by \(L_n+L_{n-1}\) for \(n\ge1\), using the same taper and modulation in both terms. The Poisson multiplier and the limiting diagonal above acquire factors \(1+r_{i\gamma}(0)^{-1}\) and \(|1+r_{i\xi}(0)^{-1}|^2\), respectively. The latter is nonzero on the retained high-frequency interval. The first-order tie splitting on genuine right zeros suffices: equality of the constant and linear terms of the score \(((1-\delta)^2+(\gamma-u)^2)/\delta\) for distinct poles forces \(\gamma_1/\delta_1=\gamma_2/\delta_2\) and \(\gamma_1\gamma_2+\delta_1\delta_2=1\). The same-sign ordinates of right zeros are above the verified height and preclude this. Hence \(n\tau_n\to\infty\) selects one dominant pole, with nonzero factor \(1+1/r_*\).

For a critical mode set \(r=r_{i\gamma}(0)\), so \((1-i\gamma)^{-1}=(1-r)/2\). The limiting coefficient of the adjacent sum contains \((1-r)(1+r^{-1})/2=(r^{-1}-r)/2\), purely imaginary. Opposite ordinates therefore cancel in pairs when all the factors \(r_{i\gamma}(\tau_n)^n\) return near one. Such simultaneous returns exist for any fixed finite list. In fact \(n\theta_\gamma(\tau_n)=n\theta_\gamma(0)+\theta_\gamma'(0)\log n+o(1)\). The continuous compact-torus flow at the finitely many speeds \(\theta_\gamma'(0)\) has unbounded return times \(u\) near the identity (compactness, or a pigeonhole argument). Near integers of size \(e^u\), the second summands then vary as slowly as required over any fixed number of integers. The discrete rotation with the speeds \(\theta_\gamma(0)\) has relatively dense returns to any neighborhood of identity in its orbit closure, by compactness and a finite cover by forward translates. One of these integer returns can be selected near each sufficiently large \(e^u\) while the log phases remain near identity. The two-polynomial scalar thus approaches zero cofinally on the genuine critical component. The same positive-diagonal argument gives the unshifted cofinal equivalence for this second observable. This last recurrence argument alone does not claim a uniform positive lower density.
