# Stable modes and the lower edge of the Poisson truncation

**Review of the Poisson localization, 19 November 2026.** This supplies an all-order estimate for individual unsmoothed Taylor partial sums of stable modes and shows that the retained scalar loses every higher-prime-power contribution asymptotically, with the full contribution paid. Symbols for the actual source and the one-quantity test are as in [the Cayley note](SINGLE_SCALAR_CAYLEY.md).

For the native critical mode \(p=i\gamma\) fix \(y=\gamma-\tau\), \(\lambda>0\), and form
\[
 f_n(w)=\sqrt2 h(w)r(w)^n,
 \quad h(w)=[1+\lambda(1-w)-iy]^{-1},\qquad
 r(w)=1-2h(w).
\]
The coefficient of \(w^j\) is the Laplace pairing with the time weight \(e^{-\lambda t}(\lambda t)^j/j!\). Thus summing orders \(j<m\) multiplies the mode by the Poisson factor \(Q_m(\lambda t)\) inside the physical integral. Put \(D=w\partial_w\) and \(v=(Dr/r)(1)=2\lambda/(1+y^2)\). Uniformly for \(y\) in any fixed compact range and \(w=e^{i\theta}\), \(-\pi\le\theta\le\pi\),
\[
 |r(w)|\le e^{-c\theta^2},\qquad |Dr(w)-vr(w)|\le C|\theta|.
\]
The first bound follows from \(1-|r(w)|^2=4\lambda(1-\cos\theta)|h(w)|^2\), bounding the denominator above on the compact set. The second follows by differentiability and the equality at \(w=1\). The constants may depend on \(\lambda\) and the ordinate bound. Both \(h\) and \(Dh\) are uniformly bounded there. Gaussian integration now gives \(\|f_n\|_{L^2(\mathbb T)}^2=O(n^{-1/2})\), \(\|(D-nv)f_n\|_{L^2(\mathbb T)}^2=O(n^{1/2})\), the latter using \(n r^{n-1}(Dr-vr)\) without division by a potential zero of \(r\).

If \(c_j=[w^j]f_n\), Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz with weights \(1+(j-nv)^2/n\) yield \(\sum_{j\ge0}|c_j|=O(1)\). Indeed the weighted sum of squares is \(O(n^{-1/2})\) and the sum of reciprocal weights is \(O(\sqrt n)\). This handles even the transition \(m\sim nv\). If all retained orders satisfy \(j/n\le v-\epsilon\) for some fixed \(\epsilon>0\), the stronger estimate \(\sum_{0\le j<m}|c_j|=O_\epsilon(n^{-1/4})\) follows by bounding \(|j-nv|\ge\epsilon n\), using the second Parseval bound and the square root of the number of retained terms. No zero-spacing or random-phase premise enters.

Take the proposed \(\lambda=10^6\), \(m=\lceil2.27\cdot10^{-19}n\rceil\), \(k=\lceil m/100\rceil\), \(\overline Q=k^{-1}\sum_{l=m+1}^{m+k}Q_l\), at sufficiently large orders. All retained indices satisfy \(j/n<2.30\cdot10^{-19}\). For every critical zero of \(|\gamma|\le2.9\cdot10^{12}\), \(v>2.37\cdot10^{-19}\) uniformly over \(0\le\tau\le1\). Hence the *entire contribution of these finitely many native modes to the retained observable tends to zero*. The same result at \(\gamma=0\) removes the constant \(1/32\) from the higher-power field. Every \(L^2\) remainder contributes \(o(1)\) because \(\overline Q(\lambda t)\to1\) pointwise, \(0\le\overline Q\le1\), \(\tau_n\to0\), and the unmodified Laguerre functions are orthonormal. By (6) in the Cayley note the total contribution of **all** higher prime powers therefore tends to zero unconditionally.

For stable critical modes above this range, averaging the Taylor partial sums from \(m+1\) through \(m+k\) gives a difference of two Fejér sums of \(f_n\): its modulus is at most \(((m+k)+m)\|f_n\|_\infty/k\le201\sqrt2\). This estimate is uniform in the ordinate. The actual source's unamplified bin weights obey \(3000(j+2)^{15}e^{-j/32}\) for \(j=\lfloor\sqrt{|\gamma|}\rfloor\ge\lfloor\sqrt{2.9\cdot10^{12}}\rfloor\), by the same sinc-product and counting proof as the [fixed-sieve bounds](../../step4-correlation-2026-11-19/FIXED_SIEVE_SIGN.md). For example their total times \(201\sqrt2\) is below \(e^{-50000}\): for \(j\ge1702938\), \(15\log(j+2)<j/4096\) with increasing slack, and the remaining geometric series costs less than a factor 40. Hence if \(d=0\), the limsup of the modulus of the retained observable is below one. The finite verified low zeros were separated before taking this limsup. Contributions beyond the taper are handled by the independent highpass argument in the parent work.

The constants in that highpass comparison have positive slack. In \(z=\lambda(1-w)\), \(|w|=1.01\) implies \(\Re z\ge-10000\), \(|\Im z|\le1.01\cdot10^6\). For \(|\gamma|>3\cdot10^{12}\), the maximum log gain per order is at most \(2(10000+0.374)/(3\cdot10^{12}-1.01\cdot10^6-1)^2\), less than \(2.224\cdot10^{-21}\). The geometric Taylor gain is \(2.27\cdot10^{-19}\log(1.01)>2.258\cdot10^{-21}\). At \(t\ge1.01(m+k)/\lambda\), the Poisson lower-tail rate \(\lambda[1-1/1.01-(\log1.01)/1.01]\) exceeds 49, so the proposed simpler \(e^{-40t}\) upper applies. Hard-cutting the prime sum at \(\log p\ge1.01(m+k)/\lambda+2\) pays an exponentially small tail by \(|\ell_n|\le\sqrt2 e(n+1)\) and elementary weighted counting. With the lower cutoff \(a n^{1/5}\), for \(t\le a n^{1/5}+s\) the change from the original per-pulse bound costs at most \((e\lambda t/m)^m\) from the opposite Poisson tail. The extra error is negligible even after absolute prime summation. Raising the lower endpoint instead to \(n/\log(n+2)\) pays a subexponential error, which suffices for the root-growth test but by itself does not preserve a constant-threshold criterion.

The scale of the upper cutoff has a mathematical explanation. For any fixed test mode \(e^{pt}\) with \(\delta=\Re p>0\), the mass of its Laguerre integral retained on \([0,\kappa n]\), even with an additional multiplier bounded by one, is at most \(\sqrt{\kappa n}e^{\delta\kappa n}\) by Cauchy–Schwarz. The complete response to this mode has exponential base \(|r_p(0)|\). Preserving the mode up to bounded error therefore entails \(\kappa\ge\log|r_p(0)|/\delta=2/\gamma^2+O(\gamma^{-4})\) at large ordinate. For the actual full field a corresponding necessary inequality under \(d>0\) is \(\kappa\ge\log R/d\), from \(|P|\ll_j e^{dt}\). The single-mode sensitivity near the verified height is \(2/H_0^2\approx2.22\cdot10^{-25}\); the proposed \(2.33\cdot10^{-25}\) cutoff is close on that scale. This comparison makes no assertion that such a zero exists or that the true field has an extremal mode at the verified boundary.
