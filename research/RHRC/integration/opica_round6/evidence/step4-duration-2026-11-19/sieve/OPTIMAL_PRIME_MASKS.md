# Optimal cancellation by adding squarefree graph layers up to a prescribed prime size

**DERIVED on paper, 19 November 2026.** Among all extensions of the prime prefix to primes of size at most \(p^K\), there is a sharp lower bound on the remaining grouped energy. Deterministic sets of actual primes attain the bound. For two layers the complete non-prefix regrouping removes an exponential fraction of the original energy. It leaves a growing positive remainder. This determines the extent to which this particular operation can supply the missing fixed-cutoff covariance.

Use the unchanged one-copy \(U,P,E_B,L_B\) and finite squarefree filter from [the individual prime theorem](INDIVIDUAL_PRIME_ECHO.md). Fix an integer \(K\ge1\), let \(p\to\infty\) through primes, \(B_p=\{q:q<p\}\), \(h=1/2\), \(\ell=\log p\), \(A=\sum_{q<p}q^{-1/2}\sim 2\sqrt p/\ell\). Each mask \(M_p\) contains \(B_p\), and all its additional primes belong to \([p,p^K]\). There is no restriction on their number, on their total Euler mass, or on how they are chosen. Then
\[
 \liminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log L_{M_p}}{A}
 \ \ge\ \sec\!\left(\frac{\pi}{K+2}\right). \tag{1}
\]
For each \(K\) an explicitly specified sequence of masks attains equality as a full limit. For \(K=1,2,3\) the three sharp values are \(2,\sqrt2,\sqrt5-1\). They decrease to 1 as \(K\) increases. For \(K=2\) the ratio of optimal remaining energy to the base energy is \(\exp(-(2-\sqrt2+o(1))A)\).

More generally, if the largest permitted new prime is \(p^\gamma\) for **any fixed real** \(\gamma\ge1\), the sharp constant is \(\sec(\pi/(\lfloor\gamma\rfloor+2))\). The integer atoms in (2) then stop at \(\lfloor\gamma\rfloor\), and the construction for that degree fits inside \(p^\gamma\). All auxiliary size estimates below can use the larger integer \(\lceil\gamma\rceil\). In particular, extending a prefix through \(p^{1.99}\) cannot lower its leading optimal log energy. Allowing \(p^2\) changes it from \(2A\) to \(\sqrt2 A\).

## Universal bound for arbitrary mask choices

First consider masks with \(\sum_{q\in M_p}q^{-1/2}=O(A)\). Give \(\lambda_q=\log q/\ell\) mass \(q^{-1/2}/A\) for all \(q\in M_p\). The weak limits of the prefix alone equal \(\delta_1\) by PNT; all primes \(q<p^{1-\delta}\) have negligible total mass for fixed \(\delta>0\). Along any subsequence one can extract a further weak limit \(\mu\) for the full bounded measures on \([0,K]\). Thus \(\operatorname{supp}\mu\subset[1,K]\), \(\mu(\{1\})\ge1\).

For fixed real \(x\), at Fourier frequency \(x/\ell\) the normalized log Euler modulus tends to \(-F(x)=-\int\cos(\lambda x)d\mu(\lambda)\) by the Euler log expansion. Its uniform quadratic remainder \(O_K(\log p)\) is \(o(A)\). Convergence holds uniformly for \(x\) on each compact interval, since the normalized forms have bounded first derivative in \(x\). The entire actual \(\widehat U\) is nonzero and has at most a finite-order zero at the origin. Integrating on any short interval of \(x\) away from zero costs at most \(O(\log\ell)\) in the log lower. The squarefree density factor costs \(O_K(\log p)\), using the crude harmonic upper up to \(p^K\). Consequently \(\liminf \log L_{M_p}/(2A)\ge\sup_x[-F(x)]\) along this further subsequence.

Put \(D=\sup_x[-F(x)]\). For every fixed \(x\), take the Cesàro average of \(D+F(x+2\pi j)\ge0\) over \(j=0,\dots,N-1\). The inner geometric sum is bounded by one and tends to zero for each noninteger \(\lambda\). Dominated convergence therefore gives a nonnegative real trigonometric polynomial
\[
 D+\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_k\cos(kx)\ge0,\qquad
 a_k=\mu(\{k\}),\quad a_1\ge1.                                    \tag{2}
\]
Here averaging has retained all integer atoms without placing any distributional assumption on the remaining primes. Fejér's coefficient inequality gives \(a_1\le2D\cos(\pi/(K+2))\). Indeed a nonnegative trigonometric polynomial of degree \(\le K\) is \(|\sum_{j=0}^{K}b_j e^{ijx}|^2\) by Fejér–Riesz factorization. The constant term is \(\sum_j|b_j|^2=D\), while \(a_1/2=\operatorname{Re}\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}b_{j+1}\bar b_j\). The latter is the quadratic form of the path matrix with entries \(1/2\) on each neighboring diagonal. Its top eigenvalue is \(\cos(\pi/(K+2))\), by the elementary Dirichlet sine basis. For the factorization, one may multiply the trigonometric polynomial by the suitable power of \(z=e^{ix}\): its nonzero roots occur in reciprocal conjugate pairs, and unit-circle roots have even multiplicity by nonnegativity. Taking one root from each pair and half of each unit multiplicity gives the factor, with a positive scalar fixed at a nonzero point. This proves (1) on all subsequences.

## Constructing genuine prime masks which attain the bound

Set \(\omega=\pi/(K+2)\), \(b_j=\sin((j+1)\omega)\) for \(0\le j\le K\), \(r_k=\sum_{j=0}^{K-k}b_j b_{j+k}\), \(a_k=r_k/r_1\) for \(1\le k\le K\). All \(a_k>0\), \(a_1=1\), and \(r_1/r_0=\cos\omega\). Put \(D_*=r_0/(2r_1)=1/(2\cos\omega)\). Then
\[
 D_*+\sum_{k=1}^K a_k\cos(kx)
 =\frac1{2r_1}\left|\sum_{j=0}^K b_j e^{ijx}\right|^2.
 \tag{3}
\]
The minimum on the right is exactly zero: the sine-vector polynomial equals \(\sin\omega\,(1+z^{K+2})/(1-2z\cos\omega+z^2)\), which has \(K\) unit-circle roots after cancellation.

For \(2\le k\le K\) scan the actual primes \(m\) in \([p^{k-1/4},p^k]\) in increasing order. Assign the individual weight \(w_k(m)=a_k m^{-h(1-1/k)}\), eventually between zero and one. Select a prime exactly when adding its weight increases the floor of the cumulative weights in this slab. Include all these selected primes and the whole prefix \(q<p\). Slabs are disjoint. The signed difference of the selected count and the accumulated weight is between \(-1\) and zero at every upper endpoint.

Let \(T=p^{11/10}\). Uniformly for \(|\tau|\le T\), partial summation bounds the error from this rounding when applied to \(m^{-1/2-i\tau}\) by \(O((1+T)p^{-(k-1/4)/2})=o(A)\), already for \(k=2\). The omitted primes below \(p^{k-1/4}\) in the corresponding weighted Euler sum cost \(O_{k}((p^{k-1/4})^{h/k})=o(A)\). Apply the [uniform twisted prime estimate](INDIVIDUAL_PRIME_ECHO.md) with upper bound \(X=p^k\) and exponent \(1-h/k\) in place of \(u\). It is valid for all real sufficiently large endpoints by the same half-integer Perron proof. Together with the prefix formula this gives, uniformly in this whole band,
\[
 \frac1A\sum_{m\in M_p}m^{-1/2-i\tau}
 =\sum_{k=1}^{K} a_k
 \frac{h}{h-ik\tau}e^{-ik\tau\ell}+o(1).                              \tag{4}
\]
At \(\tau=0\), (4) gives Euler mass \((\sum a_k+o(1))A\). Formula (4) is the literal prime sum over the common mask.

To control every frequency simultaneously, take a real variable \(s\) of density \(h e^{-hs}\) on \([0,\infty)\). The real part of the right side of (4), before its error, is \(\mathbb E\sum_{k=1}^K a_k\cos(k(\tau\ell-\tau s))\). By (3) this is at least \(-D_*\). The result is a uniform upper \((D_*+o(1))A\) for the log Euler modulus on \(|\tau|\le T\). At a fixed minimizer \(x\) of (3), frequencies near \(x/\ell\) recover the lower as in the previous section. For \(|\tau|>T\), Euler absolute values cost at most \(\exp(C_K A)\), while the actual full drive gives squared Fourier tail \(O_j(\exp(-c_j\sqrt T))\). Here \(\sqrt{T}=p^{11/20}\gg A\). Plancherel and the \(o(A)\) log density cost prove
\[
 \log L_{M_p}=(2D_*+o(1))A.
\]
This proves attainability with all mixed products among the actual selected primes retained.

## Transfer to native horizons

Fix \(0<a<2\), and choose \(p\) the first prime at or above \(n^a\) at each integer \(n\); \(p\sim n^a\). Both lower and sharp equality transfer to \(\log E_{M_p}(t)/A\), uniformly on \(n-2\log n\le t\le n\), including every native quadrature node. First take masks of mass \(\sum_{q\in M_p}q^{-1/2}\le C A\). They have \(E_{M_p}(t)=L_{M_p}+O_{a,C,K,j}(e^{-c n})\) for some positive \(c\). All sieving primes have size \(\le p^K\), a fixed power of \(n\); Bonferroni with \(O(n/\log n)\) terms controls the squarefree counting discrepancy on \(t-\log r\le n/2\). The filter variation costs \(\exp(O_{C}(A))=\exp(o(n))\) by translation Minkowski. On the remaining tail use a small fixed \(\delta>0\) with \(a(1/2+K\delta)<1\). The absolute Euler log with weights \(q^{-1/2+\delta}\) is at most \(C p^{K\delta}A=o(n)\). The actual superexponential \(U\) bound gives pointwise filter cost \(\exp(-\delta n/2+o(n))\); active discrete harmonic mass is \(O(n)\). These are the estimates of the [squarefree sampling proof](../../step4-correlation-2026-11-19/divisor/FIXED_SIEVE_RECURRENCE_AUDIT.md) with the new mask. They are uniform under the given mass bound.

## Dense extra layers cannot improve the optimum

Write \(H_\sigma=\sum_{q\in M_p}q^{-1/2-\sigma}\). If \(H_0/A\to\infty\), then \(\log L_{M_p}/A\to\infty\) and \(\log E_{M_p}(t)/A\to\infty\) uniformly on the native windows above. Together with the bounded-mass proof this covers arbitrary sequences, by extracting bounded subsequences or subsequences tending to infinity in the mass ratio.

For the translation limit use \(\sigma=0\). For native times fix \(\epsilon_K=1/(8e^K)\) and choose \(0\le\sigma\le1/2\) with \(H_\sigma=\min(H_0,\epsilon_K n/\ell)\). Such a choice exists eventually by continuity, since \(H_{1/2}\le1+K\ell\). Both selections have \(H_\sigma/A\to\infty\) because \(A=o(n/\ell)\). The normalized masses \(q^{-1/2-\sigma}/H_\sigma\) lie on \(\lambda_q\in[1,K]\) except for the prefix, which weighs at most \(A/H_\sigma=o(1)\).

Consider the even probability density proportional to \(x^2e^{-x^2/4}\) on the real line. Its cosine transform equals \((1-2\lambda^2)e^{-\lambda^2}\), strictly negative with a fixed margin on \([1,K]\). The expected normalized cosine sum is therefore \(\le-c_K\) for a fixed positive \(c_K\). Truncate \(|x|\) to a finite bound depending only on \(K\), losing less than \(c_K/4\). There must be some bounded \(x\) with the cosine sum \(\le-c_K/2\); such an \(x\) is bounded away from zero since \(\lambda_q\le K\). The cosine sum has Lipschitz constant at most \(K\), so the same conclusion with a smaller margin holds on a fixed-width interval. At \(\tau=x/\ell\) throughout that interval the weighted Euler product thus has log modulus \(\ge c'_K H_\sigma-O_K(\ell)\). The source lower bound on this band is uniform also in \(\sigma\in[0,1/2]\): the nonzero entire \(\widehat U\) has only finitely many zeros near that compact real segment. A sufficiently thin fixed strip excludes all nonreal zeros there, and the imaginary part of \(s=\sigma+i\tau\) has magnitude between fixed positive multiples of \(1/\ell\), paying at worst a fixed power of \(\ell\) for all the real zeros. Plancherel gives \(\int |e^{-\sigma v}V_{M_p}(v)|^2dv\ge\exp(c''_K H_\sigma)\) eventually. This proves the continuum claim with \(\sigma=0\) after the negligible density cost.

For the time claim, let \(W(v)=e^{-\sigma v}V_{M_p}(v)\). Throughout \(v\ge n/2\), uniform tilted bounds for the actual \(U\) and \(\log q\le K\ell\) give
\[
 |W(v)|\le C_j e^{-v/\ell}\prod_{q\in M_p}(1+q^{-1/2-\sigma+1/\ell})
 \le C_j\exp(-v/\ell+e^K H_\sigma).
\]
The integral of \(W^2\) there costs \(O_j(\ell\exp[-n/\ell+2e^KH_\sigma])=o(1)\). On \(0\le v\le n/2\) the \(L^2\) variation cost for \(W^2\) is at most \(C_j\exp(2H_\sigma)\) by the translation product for \(W,W'\), uniformly in \(\sigma\). Squarefree sampling of this truncated nonnegative \(W^2\) incurs absolute error \(O(e^{-c n})\), since \(H_\sigma=O(n/\ell)\), the discrepancy was exponentially small, and the primes are still bounded by \(p^K\). In the exact positive rough-core sum \(V_{M_p}(v)^2\ge W(v)^2\) here. After multiplication by the density, whose log costs \(O_K(\ell)\), this proves \(E_{M_p}(t)\ge\exp(c'''_K H_\sigma)\) uniformly. No sampling comparison for the potentially huge undamped whole filter was required.

In fact the sharp native-window optimum holds along **every** sequence \(p,n\to\infty\) with \(p\le\eta_K n^2\) for some fixed \(\eta_K>0\). To verify the extension, first choose \(C_K\) so large that when \(H_\sigma\ge C_K A\), the prefix in the Gaussian test occupies small enough mass to preserve half its fixed negative margin, and the resulting log-energy lower, after the density cost, exceeds \(3A\). Increase \(C_K\) also to cover the constructed mask. Then choose \(\eta_K\) so small that \(C_K A\le\epsilon_K n/\ell\) for all sufficiently large permitted \(p,n\); this uses \(A\sim2\sqrt p/\ell\). When \(H_0\ge C_K A\), the capped tilt above retains \(H_\sigma\ge C_K A\) and gives a lower exceeding the required sharp optimum. When \(H_0<C_K A\), take \(\sigma=0\) in the displayed tail bound. Both the continuous and the discrete tail are \(o(1)\) because \(2e^K C_K A\le n/(4\ell)\) and the active discrete mass costs \(O(n)\). On the remaining interval the variation costs \(\exp(O(n/\ell))\), and the squarefree discrepancy still saves \(\exp(-c_K n)\) since all primes are \(\le n^{2K}\) eventually. Thus \(E_{M_p}=L_{M_p}+o(1)\) uniformly in this bounded-mass case. These two estimates prove the stronger range, including all \(p=o(n^2)\). They assert existence of \(\eta_K>0\), with no claimed optimal quadratic constant.

Thus a fully specified graph regrouping at layers \(p^2,\ldots,p^K\) cancels a fraction \(1-\exp(-[2-\sec(\pi/(K+2))+o(1)]A)\) of the actual prefix same-core energy, for every \(K\ge2\). Even the best such operation leaves at least \(\exp((\sec(\pi/(K+2))-o(1))A)\). The restriction to finite \(K\) matters: primes beyond every fixed polynomial scale and the complementary signed sector between still-distinct cores lie outside this optimization. This result concerns the grouping of the true signed source. It does not compare \(P(t)^2\) with the \(Y=10^7\) floor at cofinal whole windows. No new formal theorem or Step 4 closure is claimed.

There is an unconditional signed consequence at common arbitrarily late **real instants**. The [real-axis argument for the genuine field](../../step4-correlation-2026-11-19/divisor/FIXED_SIEVE_RECURRENCE_AUDIT.md) gives \(t_j\to\infty\) with \(P(t_j)\to0\). Use \(n_j=\lceil t_j\rceil\) and any admissible \(p_j\to\infty\). On this **single sequence**, simultaneously for *all* the allowed masks, the complete signed sum between still-distinct rough cores equals \(-E_M(t_j)+o(1)\), with minimum possible \(E_M(t_j)\) of the precise exponential size in (1). Its relative cancellation error is at most \(\exp(-[\sec(\pi/(K+2))-o(1)]A)\). When \(p_j\) is a sufficiently small fixed multiple of \(n_j^2\) up to a prime-ratio \(1+o(1)\), this forces signed cancellation of order at least \(\exp(c_K n_j/\log n_j)\) across every one of these groupings at the same instants. The starting times are independent of the masks. By the derivative bound in the recurrence audit, common intervals of length \(\gg_j e^{-d t_j}\), with \(d\) the zero-displacement supremum, retain \(|P|\le2\) and hence a bounded absolute cancellation error. One may use \(n_j=\lceil t_j\rceil+1\) to keep both endpoints in its native terminal interval. Promoting these intervals to a complete cofinal terminal window at the original fixed cutoff remains the unresolved duration step.
