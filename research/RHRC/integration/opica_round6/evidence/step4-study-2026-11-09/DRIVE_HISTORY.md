## What this interval actually changed

The main progress is a reduction in unnecessary obligations, coupled with increasingly source-specific falsifications of proposed shortcuts:

1. The original fixed compact source can witness every zero multiplicity. A centered adjoint polynomial removes the multiplicity-dependent polynomial loss. The resulting eventual lower is (c_\rho e^{2\delta T}), with the source fixed independently of the zero and multiplicity. The earlier filtered-source family is therefore optional for this route.
2. Integer-density contributions, Type I terms, grouped diagonals and several boundary pieces have independent affordable budgets. The remaining difficulty is a complete signed arithmetic interaction, not those already-paid pieces.
3. Actual prime and semiprime components can be exponentially large and require cancellation by their actual complement. Later actual-source certificates show that compensation can also cross prime bands. Controlling separated pieces by independent nonnegative allowances can destroy the necessary credit.
4. Completion/recovery, Gaussian regularization, Abel damping and fixed-origin summatory defects are useful exact formulations. The reports also identify when their proposed estimates simply encode the original zero ceiling or RH-strength bound.
5. The canonical full moment slice can be reduced, on paper, to a smaller source-selected core with its entire operator leakage controlled. Exact finite certificates support this route. Neither the compression nor finite positivity provides the missing all-scale signed estimate.

These conclusions should supersede prompts asking merely for more vanishing moments, another exact recovery identity, a small bound for each raw prime sector, or extension of a fixed positive high-frequency gap.

## Completion, regularization and false stability

Selberg integer conjugacy proves an exact conjugacy to time multiplication, but the conjugacy is nonunitary. Positivity in the conjugated space does not establish positivity in physical energy. Its fixed-frequency nonaccretivity check blocks the generic operator inference, not the desired source-specific estimate.

The generalized-prime falsifier preserves many proposed abstract ingredients—positive generalized prime input, divisor and Selberg identities, signed convolution inversion and a rapidly decaying drive—while retaining a rightward zero and a growing response. It deliberately lacks the exact ordinary integer floor and full ordinary-zeta structure. It demonstrates which properties alone are insufficient; it is not a counterexample to ordinary primes or RH.

The literal staircase attack uses the actual floor kernel. A truncated three-cell kernel has a certified rightward zero although the complete kernel is nonzero in the same tested disk; the omitted tail removes the apparent zero. Positive finite kernels and small tail mass therefore do not settle full inverse stability.

The shift-cyclicity, global Gram and weighted-zeta separation reports distinguish a genuine finite approximation improvement from a zero-error limit. Integer-log shifts have a positive approximation obstruction; 505 rational dilations beat that obstruction with a complete tail budget. A finite residual around 0.0086 neither proves density of the full shift range nor excludes high zeros. The target is the correct first-cell profile, not the original signed source with intentional transform zeros.

The Gaussian recovery, cutoff-growth identity, Gaussian converse and Abel threshold chain is especially important for avoiding circular progress. In the reports, if (Theta) is the supremal zeta-zero real part, the regularized growth indicators satisfy
$
\Gamma_q=(2\Theta-1)/q,qquad

These are DERIVED paper identifications, not newly compiled statements. An external improved ceiling supplies the corresponding better regularization bound, but cannot bootstrap itself through an equality which retains the same ceiling. A polynomial Gaussian bound would suffice, yet it was not proved. Qualitative Abel (L^2) membership is a weaker proposed gate; a sequence of damping values is needed to avoid exceptional numerator cancellations.

Finite Euler tails shows why prefix agreement is insufficient: finite actual-prime inverses introduce a growing artificial mode. Exact pole subtraction repairs finite (L^2) membership, but its norms diverge as the Euler cutoff grows. This falsifies those approximations as a uniformly bounded passage to the limit, not the actual infinite object.

## Arithmetic bridge and the fixed-origin obstruction

The weighted summatory bridge supplies a more directly arithmetic gate:
$
f_\lambda(n)=\prod_{p\mid n}(1-p^{-\lambda}),\quad
R_\lambda(x)=\sum_{n\le x}f_\lambda(n)-x/\zeta(1+\lambda),
$
$
\int_1^\infty |R_\lambda(x)|^2x^{-2}\,dx<\infty.
$
It transports this exact signed discrepancy to the completed Abel observable with an explicitly bounded operator. A sequence of such bounds approaching zero damping would be sufficient, but no such sequence is independently established.

Nonperiodic divisor separation and optimized cutoff improve how much of the small-divisor sector is affordable. They retain the constant and continuous tails. The remaining number of cofactors may be small relative to the original system, yet its actual prime component can have large energy requiring complement cancellation. Counting the residual indices does not estimate their signed sum.

Tail-free dilation cancels density and the infinite arithmetic tail in an exact dilation defect. Its diagonal is finite for every fixed positive damping; the remaining finite off-diagonal needs a one-sided complete bound.

Stationary covariance obtains a good translation-averaged variance, then explicitly tests transfer to the required arithmetic origin. The loss is quantified rather than hidden: the optimized bound at (lambda=1/3) has exponent (38/27), worse than the existing PNT exponent (4/3). “Almost every starting phase” is not information about the required fixed phase. A source-specific fixed-origin estimate remains OPEN.
