# Removing a class of surviving vertices with shifted primes in the same cofactor

**DERIVED on paper.** There exists a positive-density class of the *actual surviving* squarefree rough vertices, strictly beyond the original donor ceiling and satisfying the running-count, gap and corrected-histogram restrictions, that can also be removed on every sufficiently late whole terminal shell. All new opposite-sign partners are distinct actual survivors. The change in either original weighted one-sided node energy is \(O(1)\) per shell, with absolute differences summed over nodes. This conclusion uses a new deterministic selection on top of the old selection. Its joint signed remainder remains **OPEN**.

Define the true intermediate cofactors

    a=d q_1\cdots q_{r-1},\qquad U^{a_i}<q_i\le U^{b_i}\quad(q_i\text{ prime}).

The unmarked targets are all \(aP\) in the shell with \(P\) prime. For each **same \(a\)**, candidates are every \(a\ell P'\) in the shell for \(\ell\in\mathcal I_2\) and \(P'\) prime. Both \(P,P'\) are uniquely largest. Both intermediate cofactors lie in \((U^{\beta/4},U^\beta]\) eventually, hence strictly exceed the old \(X^\alpha\) donor ceiling, since \(U>X^{.99}\). The product of all factors other than the largest is either \(a\) or \(a\ell\); the earlier central divisor gap follows because every divisor is at most \(U^\beta\) or at least \(U^{1-\beta}\). The other targets of the previous selector are ruled out by the good-core properties. In particular \(D_{U^{1/u}}(n)\) is exactly \(d\) or \(d\ell\) and there are exactly \(r\) factors above that split. The large primes lying in any of the moving tests change a count by at most their number. Their log-product contribution at any particular threshold is at most their number times the log of that threshold. These are the slack bounds included when selecting the core. Thus everything offered is present among the **actual** previous survivors, regardless of previous partner ordering.

The map from a source vertex back to its \(a\), and to its optional \(\ell\), is unique: remove the uniquely largest prime, then remove the only prime in \(\mathcal I_2\) if present. The intermediate boxes are separated and above every core factor. The signs are \(-\mu(a)\) for the target, \(+\mu(a)\) for any candidate of the same \(a\). The cofactor ranges can be narrow; their density constants then depend on the chosen boxes and tolerance.

## Uniform true prime supply on native cells

Use original integer widths \(H_1=2^{\lfloor(\log_2 H_0)/2\rfloor},\ H_2=2^{\lfloor(.5+\eta)\log_2U\rfloor},\ H_3=2^{\lfloor .58\log_2U\rfloor}\), where \(H_0=U2^{-\lceil\sqrt{\lfloor\log_2X\rfloor}\rceil}\) on all sufficiently late complete shells. For each cofactor \(t\le U^\beta\) the scaled prime interval has length \(H_i/t\) and location \(V=U/t\). At the first two stages the native exceptional-window theorem with \(v_1=.49,\ v_2=.5+\eta/2\) bounds the *original integer cell length* defective for any one \(t\) by \(O_\xi(t(U/t)^{\nu(v_i)+\xi})\) for fixed relative tolerance \(1/10\). Indeed the proofs integrate the variable-window prime sum on the actual rescaled cell and remove prime powers. With \(t=U^\lambda,\ 0\le\lambda\le\beta\), the two length powers relative to \(V\) are \((.5-\lambda-o(1))/(1-\lambda)\) and \((.5+\eta-\lambda-o(1))/(1-\lambda)\). Both exceed the stated \(v_i\) by a fixed margin uniformly. At the third stage \((.58-\lambda-o(1))/(1-\lambda)>17/30+.005\). Thus every cell at stage three has the relative prime estimate for all \(t\). Half-open convention and integer endpoints affect prime counts by at most one, absorbed by these growing lengths. [Exact grid conversion](../step4-prime-transport-2026-11-19/PRIME_SPLIT.md) · [Gafni and Tao, Lemma 1.1 and Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24017) · [Guth and Maynard, Corollary 1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20552)

Taking the union bound over **all integers** \(t\le U^\beta\) costs at most \(O_\xi(U^{\nu(v_i)+\beta(2-\nu(v_i))+\xi})\). This includes every \(a\) and \(a\ell\) simultaneously, independently of which cofactors qualify as good cores. Since \(\nu(.49)<1-4c,\ \nu(.5+\eta/2)<.75\), the exceptional lengths are \(K_1=O(U^{1-3c}),\ K_2=O(U^{.76})\) for sufficiently small fixed \(\xi\). The sum is an estimate for total integer length, so it also bounds the entire number of unmatched targets even if all vertices of every defective cell are counted.

On any good cell of width \(H_i\), the number of \(aP\) is at most \(1.1H_i/(a\log(U/a))\) and at least \(.9H_i/(a\log(U/a))\) for each \(a\). Its complete genuine same-\(a\) pool has at least
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal I_2}{.9H_i\over a\ell\log(U/(a\ell))}
       \ge {1.8H_i\over a\log(U/a)} .
\]
The fixed slack covers all eligible \(a\) individually. The lower target count and the reciprocal mass imply \(\gg U/\log y\) prescribed targets per whole shell, also for either separate fixed parity of \(d\). The same elementary rough sieve bounds all native vertices by \(O(U/\log y)\).

## Actual pairing, costs and claim limits

At stages \(H_1,H_2,H_3\), process each cell and each \(a\) separately, pairing unused targets in increasing integer order with unused opposite candidates, in increasing integer order. In a good parent each previously finished pair debited one of each of its two *same-\(a\)* lists. Lists for different \(a\)'s never collide. No candidate is a prescribed target. Every third-stage cell is good, hence all targets have distinct actual partners. Retain the identity of every unused candidate in the new survivor set. The new sets contain every designated unmarked target; selecting all potential marked candidates would give a different signed field.

Let \(R\) be the old actual survivor set, \(R'\) its complement of just the two endpoints of every new pair, within \(R\). Let \(s_J\) be the full signed endpoint count of the new pairs in node \(J\). Then \(r_J-r'_J=s_J\). For \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\) the absolute change in \([\sigma r_J]_+^2\) is at most \(2H |s_J|\). For \(H\ge H_3\), \(s_J=0\). At any single intermediate width \(H_i\le H<H_{i+1}\), all pairs finished by stage \(i\) are internal to their nodes. Therefore \(\sum_{|J|=H}|s_J|\le2K_i\), counting **both** endpoints of every deferred pair. The weighted cost of these levels is \(O(K_i H_{i+1}^2/(U H_0))\) by the geometric sum. The two exponents are \(-3c+2\eta+o(1)\) and \(.76+1.16-2+o(1)\), both negative. Below \(H_1\) the trivial count gives \(O(H_1^2/H_0)=O(1)\). Consequently for every subset of nodes in a complete shell,
\[
 \sum_J w_J\left|[\sigma r_J]_+^2-[\sigma r'_J]_+^2\right|\ll 1.
\]
The same proof applies to the ordinary squares. This norm comparison includes all cross terms with the actual unselected complement through the exact charge difference.

An optional **relative** comparison for the primitive signed pair graph starts at \(R\) and ends at \(R'\). The diagonal change costs \(O(1)\) per shell (total weighted number of selected vertices at each level is at most \(H/H_0\)). For a selected vertex \(n=tP\), \(t\le U^\beta<X^{.01}\), a distinct \(n'\) whose pair fails the GCD test must share \(P\). In a cell of length \(H\) there are at most \(2H/P\) such other multiples, including when the number is zero. Summing their absolute weights geometrically over depths gives \(O(H_0/(UP))\) per selected vertex. With \(P\ge U^{1-\beta}\), at most \(U\) selected vertices and both pair orientations, the total signed masked-covariance change costs \(O(1+2^{-s_X}U^\beta)\) in that shell. This only pays the new step; it does not silently extend an earlier unmasked-only comparison to the full primitive graph. The \(O(1)\) node estimate above needs no GCD payment.

The target family alone in a fixed parity has one sign, and already its root-cell energy is \(\gg H_0/(\log y)^2\) because every root is a union of good third-stage cells. Adding its measured same-cofactor partners pays their joint cost at all original depths. This gives a genuine critical-scale deletion beyond the old donor range. It leaves the one-sided energy of \(R'\) unbounded at the required scale. In particular, neither all large-cofactor labels nor every real shifted prime has been removed. No new formal RH or Step 4 claim follows.
