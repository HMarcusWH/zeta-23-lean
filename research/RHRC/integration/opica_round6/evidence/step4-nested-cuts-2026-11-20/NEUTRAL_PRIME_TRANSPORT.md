# What one genuine prime insertion cancels on the selected source

**DERIVED on paper, 20 November 2026.** Two disjoint sets of actual surviving vertices have opposite Möbius signs and each has order \(H/\log y\) labels on a long native cell of length \(H\). Using all primes in the first mark interval of reciprocal mass one gives the signed union an independently computable discrepancy of order \(H/\log U\), with a definite sign. Varying the number of genuinely large factors can also cancel that discrepancy to the accuracy of the prime number theorem. The remaining source and the spent partners are included explicitly below. The cofinal one-sided upper and Step 4 are **OPEN**.

\[
 m_1=\sum_{\mathcal I}\log\ell/\ell=(e-1+o(1))\log y,\qquad
 m_2=\sum_{\mathcal I}(\log\ell)^2/\ell=O((\log y)^2).
\]

First take \(r=2\) large primes. Use the same products \(m=p_1p_2\) with \(.11<\log p_1/\log m<.13\) in each of two families. ## A common positive-density cofactor set for all shifts

Use disjoint full dyads \([W,2W)\subset[U^{\tau_1},U^{\tau_2}]\). They provide \(\asymp_{\tau_1,\tau_2} L\) dyads. On each, the squarefree \(Y\)-rough population is \((1+o(1))WV(Y)\), and \((1/2+o(1))WV(Y)\) have each sign. For the unsigned count use adjacent Bonferroni truncations at \(C_A\log\log W+O(1)\). There are \(Y^{O_A(\log\log W)}=W^{o(1)}\) divisibility floor errors, the reciprocal truncation error is \(O_A((\log W)^{-A})\), and repeated prime factors cost \(O(W/Y+\sqrt W)\). For the signed count use the exact convolution \(\mu_Y=\mu*{\bf1}_{Y\text{-smooth}}\) where \(\mu_Y=\mu{\bf1}_{P^->Y}\). Through the smooth variable \(\sqrt t\) the unconditional Mertens bound gives \(tV(Y)^{-1}e^{-c\Phi(\log t)}\), with \(\Phi(L)=L^{3/5}(\log L)^{-1/5}\). The reciprocal tail costs \(t e^{-\log t/(2\log Y)}(\log Y)^{O(1)}\), by Rankin at \(1/\log Y\) and the finite Euler product. Both are \(o(WV(Y))\) at the two endpoints since \(\log Y=O(\log\log W)\). [Lee and Leong, unconditional Mertens bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06141v5)

The following deletions from the even core are estimated on **all** \(Y\)-rough \(d\) of each dyad, before imposing the sign. Choose constants to make their joint exceptional proportion below \(1/4\) relative to \(WV(Y)\). This avoids any assumption of probabilistic independence between parity and the filters.

1. For every threshold \(v\le 2W\), keep the \(d\)'s with running prime count above \(Y\) within \((K/2)\sqrt{1+\sum_{Y<p\le2W}1/p}\) of its harmonic center. The maximal fourth-moment proof in the linked running-count paper applies verbatim with threshold \(Y=(\log W)^{O(1)}\): its four-prime floor cost is \(W^{1/10+o(1)}\), its dyad length \(W\), and every small-sieve floor error \(Y^{O(\log\log W)}=W^{o(1)}\). Its excluded fraction is \(O(K^{-4})\). Extending \(v\) from \(2W\) to \(2U\) adds \(O_{\tau_1}(1)\) to the center, and changing the lower endpoint from \(Y\) to \(y\) adds at most \(b\). Up to \(r+1\) new prime factors enter at any threshold. These bounded changes fit the other half of the admissible deviation for every sufficiently late shell.
2. For every \(z_j=U^{2^{-j}}>y,\ j\ge6\), require the cofactor log-product \(\log D_{z_j}(d)\le(C/2)(j+1)^2\log z_j\). For \(Y<z_j\le W^{1/40}\), the first-moment proof of the linked split paper bounds its sum over rough \(d\) by \(O(W V(Y)\log z_j)\), by summing \(\log p\) times the true divisibility counts. At \(z_j\le Y\) this product is one. At \(z_j>W^{1/40}\), \(\log(2W)\le41\log z_j\) eventually. The exceptional proportion sums to \(O(1/C)\). Each inserted prime below \(z_j\) adds at most \(\log z_j\), so the completed label meets the full bound for all \(j\).
3. If using the three optional count intervals of the split paper, keep \(d\) with centered count within \((K/2)\sqrt{1+M_i}\) in each \(I_i\cap(Y,W^{1/40}]\), centering on that intersection. Its harmonic mass is at most the original \(M_i\). The four-divisor moment bounds the total excluded fraction by \(O(K^{-4})\). Above \(W^{1/40}\), \(d\) has at most 41 more factors, and the harmonic mass through \(2U\) is \(O_{\tau_1}(1)\). The omitted lower harmonic mass is at most \(b\). Each \(M_i\to\infty\); these bounded shifts and the \(r+1\) inserted primes fit the other half. Apply the first-moment argument also at \(z_0=\exp(\sqrt L)\) with threshold \((C/2)\log z_0\), losing only \(O(1/C)\). This supplies the optional moving product cap. The fixed-power small part is exactly \(d\) or \(d\ell\), hence is \(\le U^\delta\), and the large part has the prescribed allowed \(r\).

Take the union of the remaining even \(d\)'s over the dyads. It has \(\sum_{\mathcal D}1/d\asymp L/\log y\) by the upper and lower counts on each dyad. Both completed families pass every listed test on the *same* selector. Indeed \(P^+(n)>U^{.80}\) and \(n/P^+(n)>U^{.10}>X^{.001}\) eventually. ## Genuine PNT counts after shifting the large primes

Let \(a=d\) or \(d\ell\), and let \(B(a;J)\) count its completions. Uniformly in these \(a\) and \(J\subset[U,2U)\),
\[
 B(a;J)=C_D\int_J{dx\over a\log(x/a)}+O_D((U/a)e^{-c_D\Phi(L)}),
 \qquad C_D=\int_D {d\lambda_1\cdots d\lambda_{r-1}\over\prod_i\lambda_i}>0 .
 \tag{1}
\]
In log-prime coordinates each angular constraint is linear after multiplication by \(\sum\log p_i\), and \(x/a\in J/a\) constrains their sum to an interval. With the other primes fixed, the section in any remaining variable is an interval, possibly empty, with upper endpoint \(B\le2U/(a\prod_{i\ne j}p_i)\); at a nonempty section every prime coordinate exceeds \(U^c\) for fixed \(c>0\). Replacing one prime-counting measure at a time by \(dt/\log t\) costs \(O(B e^{-c'\Phi(L)})\) from both endpoints of the interval. For each of the remaining variables the reciprocal measure on \([U^c,2U]\), whether prime or continuous, is \(O_c(1)\). Thus all replacements cost the stated error. Separated shares prevent repeated prime coordinates. The continuum substitution \(\log p_i=\lambda_i\log(x/a)\) with \(\sum\lambda_i=1\) gives (1). This proves the required cofactor uniformity even when the endpoints of \(J\) cut through the simplex. [Johnston and Yang, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.01980v2) · [OPICA raw angular counts](../step4-parity-deficit-2026-11-20/UNBALANCED_PRIME_SHARES.md)

Fix \(1/2<\nu<3/5\). For every sufficiently large \(X\) and every \(J\) with \(H=|J|\ge Ue^{-L^\nu}\), take \(t=\log(x/d)\asymp L\). Uniformly pointwise,
\[
 \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal I}{1\over\ell(t-\log\ell)}-{1\over t}
 ={b-1\over t}+{m_1\over t^2}
     +O(m_2/t^3)=(1+o(1)){(e-1)\log y\over t^2}.
\]
Here \(L/(Y\log y)\to0\) already from \(Y>y\asymp L^2\). Summing (1) over \(\ell\) costs \(O((U/d)(1+b)e^{-c_D\Phi(L)})=o(H\log y/(d L^2))\), including both endpoints. Thus on every such cell, uniformly for every good cofactor,
\[
 \sum_{\mathcal I} B(d\ell;J)-B(d;J)
 =(1+o(1))\,{C_D(e-1)\log y\over d}
            \int_J {dx\over \log^2(x/d)}.
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular the two individual counts after summing \(d\) are each \(\asymp H/\log y\), while the genuine signed union has magnitude \(\asymp H/L\) and sign \((-1)^{r+1}\). Both individual families have positive density among the original native labels. All constants and the \(o(1)\) are uniform over the indicated late shells and cells at fixed parameters.

## Energy, complementary history and a second cancellation

Write \(H_0=U2^{-s_X}\), \(s_X=\lceil\sqrt{\lfloor\log_2 X\rfloor}\rceil\); the native node weight is \(w_J=H/(UH_0)\). On the whole tree the sum of \(w_J\) times the squared signed union charge is \(\asymp H_0/L^2\), and the two separate energies are each \(\asymp H_0/(\log y)^2\). For the upper bounds on long levels use (2) and sum the squared widths geometrically. On shorter levels use the genuine distinct-label bound \(H\); their total contribution is \(O((Ue^{-L^\nu})^2/H_0)=o(H_0/L^A)\) for every fixed \(A\). The root cells already give the lower bounds, also in the appropriate one-sided polarity. This rules out a subpower energy allowance for mere reciprocal-mass-one prime insertion, even after native selection.

On root cells the actual paid selector removes zero signed mass because every matched pair is internal to that cell. The entire remaining source thus has the full rough Möbius estimate \(r_J=O(Ue^{-c\Phi(L)})\) at its two endpoints by the convolution bound above at \(Y=y\). In particular its complementary **actual survivors** contribute the opposite of (2), summed over \(d\), up to \(o(H_0/L)\) on each root cell. At smaller depths the exact identity is always \(r_J=\mu(B_0\cap J)+\mu(B_1\cap J)+\mu(({\rm survivors}\setminus(B_0\cup B_1))\cap J)\). No step erases unmatched history, substitutes an expectation, or infers smallness for the GCD-masked graph after deleting vertices.

There is also a constructive compensating choice within the surviving source. Choose two interior angular polytopes \(D_r,D_{r+1}\) obeying the same large-prime separation and largest-share bounds, and having equal \(C_D>0\). Start with small positive-volume polytopes for both orders. Shrinking either about an interior point changes its continuous positive angular integral continuously down to zero. Pick a common mass smaller than both. Choose \(u,\mathcal D\) with room for both counts before taking \(X\) large. The two orders give disjoint labels because \(d\ell<U^{\inf\lambda_1/2}\) eventually (reduce \(\tau_2\) if necessary); their common small factor is thus recoverable. For every \(a=d\) or \(d\ell\), (1) has **exactly the same main term** for the two orders. Taking the absolute difference of the true two counts at each \(a\) and summing gives \(O(Ue^{-c'\Phi(L)})\) after the harmonic cofactor sum. The inequality survives arbitrary common multipliers of modulus at most one depending on \(a\). This cancels (2) for these two genuine opposite-sign macroscopic sectors, including after selection. It only uses this particular matching of angular volumes and a common cofactor measure.

Indeed across all levels the signed union of these four families has squared energy \(\ll H_0 e^{-c''\Phi(L)}\): on each cell use the minimum of \(H\) and \(A=Ue^{-c'\Phi(L)}\), sum all widths below \(A\) geometrically and pay \(O(\log U)\) levels above it at \(A^2/H_0\) per level. The factors \((U/H_0)^2\log U=\exp(O(\sqrt L))\) are absorbed by \(\Phi(L)\). This proves a saving at native depths for actual retained vertices; the available upper is still \(U^{1-o(1)}\). Step 4 requires a subpower upper for the *whole* actual complementary signed charge at common cofinal horizons. Neither the compensated union nor a single protected component supplies that estimate.
