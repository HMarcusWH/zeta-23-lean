**Exact restriction on using whole divisor fibres.** Let D consist of all positive y-rough integers ≤M, including 1. For an interval mask w:D→{0,1}, the expansion w(d)=∑_{n∈D:d|n} c(n) exists uniquely by descending triangular inversion. Explicitly c(n)=∑_{k:nk∈D} μ(k)w(nk), since every divisor of a member of D stays in D and ∑_{e|k}μ(e) vanishes for k>1. Thus the coefficient on the trivial fibre is c(1)=∑_{d∈D}μ(d)w(d), exactly the requested scalar cell charge. Pairing the expansion with μ annihilates every other complete fibre. In particular, if w(1)=0 and w is nonzero, a nonnegative combination of whole fibres cannot represent it, since w(1)=∑c(n). The graph path by itself therefore gives no inexpensive positive representation of the mask. This conclusion concerns this divisibility basis. A different source-specific estimate for its inverse coefficients could give new control. The actual whole coarse cell at X=1,656,575 already has c(1)=1.
