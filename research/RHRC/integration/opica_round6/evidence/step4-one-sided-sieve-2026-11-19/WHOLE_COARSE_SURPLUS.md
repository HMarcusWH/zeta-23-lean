## A fixed interior power interval suffices

**DERIVED on paper from the signed-history result.** Fix \(0.99\le u<v\le1\). Form \(V_\pm^{[u,v]}\) on the original grid using only coarse nodes in entire dyadic shells contained in \([X^u,X^v]\) and in the native terminal range. With \(b=\sup_\rho\Re\rho-1/2\), each polarity satisfies
\[
             1+V_\pm^{[u,v]}(X)=X^{2vb+o(1)} .
\]
For the upper, in a shell \(U\le X^v\) the rough Mertens estimate gives node charge \(O_\eta(U^{1/2+b+\eta}X^{o_\eta(1)})\). The sum of weights at each depth is at most \(2^s/U\); the number of depths and shells is logarithmic. Thus each energy is \(O_\eta(X^{2vb+2\eta+o_\eta(1)})\). For \(b=0\) the added one gives the other direction.

For \(b>0\) freeze the original \(X\) and \(y\) and take fixed \(0<2\sigma<v-u\). In the signed-history argument apply the finite zero identity at \(Y=X^{v-\sigma}\); the rough cost is still \(Y^{o(1)}\) since \(y\asymp(\log Y)^2\). The squared lower and the uniform pointwise upper give \(\int_0^{\log Y}|W|\ge X^{(v-\sigma)b-o(1)}\). Times below \((u+\sigma)\log X\) cost at most \(X^{(u+\sigma)b+o(1)}\), with a strictly smaller exponent. The positive xi kernel gives the same lower for both polarities on the resulting interval enlarged by \(O(\log\log X)\), using a fixed line-shift exponent smaller than \((v-\sigma)b\). The tent at these times uses only labels between \(X^{u+\sigma-o(1)}\) and \(X^{v-\sigma+o(1)}\). Every entire shell touching that support lies in the prescribed interval for large \(X\). All out-of-range nodes therefore have zero coefficient in the sign-preserving node expansion. That argument bounds the observed sign by \(X^{o(1)}\sqrt{V_\pm}\). Removing all fine nodes costs \(O(\log X)\), then \(\sigma\) can decrease to zero after taking the lower limit in \(X\). This proof requires a fixed positive gap \(v-u\). It supplies no lower on one prescribed shell of fixed ratio. ## Why the finite prime/semiprime split cannot be extrapolated

**DERIVED.** Fix a positive integer \(r\). In a whole shell \(U\asymp X\), retain only the actual squarefree \(y\)-rough labels with at most \(r\) prime factors, keeping their true signs. Their total charge has sign \((-1)^r\) eventually and absolute size
\[
          \asymp_r \frac{U}{\log U}(\log\log U)^{r-1}.
\]
Indeed, the count with exactly \(j\ge1\) prime factors has this order with \(j\) in place of \(r\). For an upper, write \(n=mp\) with \(p\) its largest prime. Then \(p\ge U^{1/j}\) and \(m\le2U^{1-1/j}\). The prime counting bound costs \(O_j(U/(m\log U))\); the sum of reciprocal products of \(j-1\) primes costs \(O_j((\log\log U)^{j-1})\). For a lower, choose \(j-1\) distinct primes freely from \((y,U^{1/(2j)}]\) and one last prime \(p\in[U/m,2U/m)\). It exceeds all the others, so the product is counted once if the smaller primes are unordered. Ordinary PNT supplies \(\gg U/(m\log U)\) choices uniformly. The small-prime reciprocal sum has order \(\log\log U\), since \(\log\log y=o(\log\log U)\); repeated prime choices cost a lower order term by \(\sum_p1/p^2<\infty\). For \(j=1\) use PNT directly. The highest retained layer dominates all the previous layers.

The \(2^s\) base cells of this complete shell all belong to the coarse tree. Cauchy's inequality gives total energy of the dominant polarity at least \(U2^{-s}(\log\log U)^{2r-2}/(C_r\log^2 U)=X^{1-o(1)}\) on this truncated source. For \(r=2\) this is an unconditional large positive energy on the prime/semiprime truncation. The full rough shell charge is \(o_A(U/\log^A U)\) for every fixed \(A\), by the existing rough Mertens bound with its unconditional logarithmic saving. Hence the omitted higher layers carry the opposing aggregate shell drift. This explains why even a verified long stretch of zero positive cells when only two layers are physically possible cannot control the eventual full-source excess. **OPEN** remains an independent cofinal subpower upper for the whole-cell excess of either polarity with every layer retained.
