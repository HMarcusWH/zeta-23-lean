# Which repository proof paths can consume a signed arithmetic estimate?

At commit `c867a8f15320ad6ac69177e0a498de4cb9d744f9`, tree `573cda1f06d2d7a0e61349a53183311e4ba621fc`, I checked five possible input theorems against eight precise targets. The calculation traversed the 16 checked-in relation shards, allowing an edge only when the compiler export records use in a declaration’s value. Of 40 provider–target pairs, two reach the target, eight have no recorded path between indexed endpoints, and 30 have an unindexed target. I then inspected the source at this commit for each target, including those outside the graph. The receipt binds the graph and source files by hash. The identity alone gives the difference of the two sides. **The smaller canonical test.** The pinned source also gives an RH equivalence requiring this inequality only on the exact retained cubic trials of the generated whole-cell certificates, beyond one fixed aperture. The generated-family equivalence and its forced-failure theorem are absent from the stored declaration list. Reading their proof bodies shows direct uses of the very same two indexed canonical energy lemmas. An off-line zero forces a genuine retained trial with the reverse strict inequality at arbitrarily large aperture. An independent domination theorem on this family would suffice. Replacing that trial by a prime-factor allocation vector needs a proved comparison of the two actual forms and their signs. **The source hyperbola.** The local proof of the eventual lower equivalence directly consumes the normalized full-quadratic identity and the uniform diagonal bound. Its native term still includes rank-one compensation and every signed off-diagonal. The final constant-lower criterion directly uses the indexed theorem giving both signed late excursions under an off-line zero. A weaker frontier consumer gives \(|\Re\rho-1/2|\le\alpha\) from an eventual lower of \(-C_\alpha e^{\alpha t}\) on the literal long term. The frontier proof needs every sufficiently large \(t\) for that fixed \(\alpha\). A bound for all positive \(\alpha\) then gives RH. Cofinality in the paper dyadic-tree test relies on its own every-horizon exponent argument. The two horizon conditions cannot be exchanged without a transfer proof.
