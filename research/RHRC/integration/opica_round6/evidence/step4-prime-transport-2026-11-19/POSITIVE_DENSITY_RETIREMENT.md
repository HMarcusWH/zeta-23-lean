# A positive fraction of the rough Möbius vertices can be removed

**DERIVED on paper, 19 November 2026.** For each fixed error exponent \(\epsilon>0\), one can delete every prime and a positive fraction of the genuine squarefree rough labels, together with explicitly chosen opposite-sign partners, at a total signed cost \(O_\epsilon(X^\epsilon)\) on the aligned sequence \(X=2^{100t}\). The comparison includes all edges with at least one deleted endpoint, both signs, every shared ancestor, and the original GCD condition. The fraction depends on the requested exponent. The signed covariance among surviving labels remains open.

## Statement on the original grid

Use the rough graph of [the rough-source comparison](ROUGH_PRIME_TRANSPORT.md) with \(y=\lceil\log_2 X\rceil^2\), and the tree geometry of [the native construction](../step4-harmonic-recovery-2026-11-19/DYADIC_TREE.md). Nonzero vertices are squarefree \(X^{.99}<n\le X\) with \(P^-(n)>y\), signed by \(\mu(n)\). A node of length \(H=H_0 2^{-k}\) in a shell \([U,2U)\) has ordered edge weight \(w_H=H/(UH_0)\), with \(H_0=U2^{-s}\) and \(s=\lceil\sqrt{\log_2 X}\rceil\) on the aligned cutoffs. Count distinct pairs with \(\gcd(n,r)<D=X^{.01}\). Write \(C(E)\) for the original signed statistic with both endpoints in \(E\). The statement holds both with all levels and with only the original coarse levels. All intervals are half open; the two aligned power endpoints have zero Möbius weight for large \(X\).

There are fixed \(0<\kappa=\alpha/100<\alpha<\min(.01,\epsilon/2)\), chosen below, for which the deletion set \(S\) contains every source label with \(n/P^+(n)\le X^\kappa\). Every other selected vertex has \(X^\kappa<n/P^+(n)\le X^\alpha\). Write \(R\) for its actual complement, including unused potential partners. Then
\[
 |C(\mathrm{all})-C(R)|\ll_\epsilon (1+2^{-s}X^\alpha)\log(2X).
 \tag{1}
\]
For sufficiently large aligned \(X\), even the prescribed targets alone contain at least \(c\kappa\) of the complete rough vertex count for an absolute \(c>0\). Thus (1) pays for a sector of positive relative density at each fixed tolerance. In the unrestricted \(\mu_6\) graph the same construction with cofactors coprime to 30 and their multiples by 5 gives positive ordinary density in the terminal range.

## Genuine target and partner counts

Put \(Z=X^\kappa\). In a shell the target vertices have form \(mp\) with \(m\le Z\) rough, squarefree and \(p\) prime; \(p\) is uniquely the largest prime for all targets and possible partners. For the partner cofactors use all squarefree \(m\) with \(Z<m\le X^\alpha/y^2\) and \(P^-(m)>y^2\). Offer both \(m\) and each \(\ell m\) for primes \(y<\ell\le y^2\). The cofactor families are disjoint and have opposite signs for each \(m\). Uniqueness of the largest prime makes their source labels disjoint. The native label sign is \(-\mu(m)\) for cofactor \(m\).

Let \(V(z)=\prod_{p\le z}(1-1/p)\). At \(z=y,y^2\), uniformly for \(u\ge X^{\kappa/2}\), the squarefree \(z\)-rough count is \((1+o(1))uV(z)\). To see the uniformity, truncate the elementary inclusion–exclusion sum at adjacent odd and even indices \(k\asymp20(1+\sum_{p\le z}1/p)\). The omitted symmetric-sum bound is \(O((\log z)^{-3})\) as a proportion. The floor errors cost \(O(z^{k+1})=X^{o(1)}\) in count. Removing squares of primes above \(z\) costs \(O(u/z)\). Mertens' product theorem supplies \(V(z)\sim e^{-\gamma}/\log z\). Reciprocal summation and \(\sum_{y<\ell\le y^2}1/\ell\to\log2\) show that **each** partner sign has reciprocal mass at least
\[
 \big(99e^{-\gamma}\log(2)/2+o(1)\big)\frac{\log Z}{\log y}.
\]
Both target signs combined have mass at most \(\prod_{y<p\le Z}(1+1/p)\le(1+o(1))\log Z/\log y\). This slack covers the fixed prime-count tolerances and \(\log(U/m)/\log U\in[1-\alpha/.99,1+o(1)]\), uniformly through the partner pools. On the last shell, ordinary PNT on \([U/m,2U/m)\) is uniform for \(m\le Z\), since \(U/Z\to\infty\). Restricting the reciprocal sum even to \(X^{\kappa/2}<m\le X^\kappa\) gives \(\gg\kappa X/\log y\) target labels. The entire rough source has \(\ll X/\log y\) labels by the same count.

## Aligned prime intervals and opposite-sign matching

Use the variable short-interval exceptional exponents \(\nu(v)\) and the real-window to integer-grid conversion proved in [the interval matching calculation](PRIME_SPLIT.md). Choose \(0<c<.002\) with \(\nu(.49)<1-4c\). Put \(\eta=c/4\), \(v_1=.49,\ v_2=.5+\eta/2\); \(\nu(v_2)\le 3(1-v_2)/2<.75\). Choose \(\lambda=\alpha/.99<\min(.001,c/40,\eta/10)\) once, further reducing \(\alpha\) for the requested error exponent. Take lengths \(H_1=2^{\lfloor(\log_2 H_0)/2\rfloor},\,H_2=2^{\lfloor(.5+\eta)\log_2U\rfloor},\,H_3=2^{\lfloor.58\log_2 U\rfloor}\). They are nested below \(H_0\) eventually. [Gafni–Tao, Definition 1.1, Lemma 1.1 and Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24017) · [Guth–Maynard, Corollary 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.20552)

For \(m\le X^\alpha\le U^\lambda\), the scaled grid at stage \(i\) has spacing \(H_i/m\) and location \(V=U/m\). For \(i=1,2\), that spacing exceeds \(V^{v_i+\sigma}\) for a fixed \(\sigma>0\) by the strict choices above. The defective cells for one cofactor occupy total original integer length \(O_\xi(m(U/m)^{\nu(v_i)+\xi})\) for every fixed \(\xi>0\). This follows by integrating the variable-window prime count over the actual scaled cell. A fixed relative failure forces a positive exceptional measure of order at least its length divided by \(\log V\); choose a smaller preliminary loss to absorb logarithms. The sum over **all** cofactors costs \(O_\xi(U^{\nu(v_i)+\lambda(2-\nu(v_i))+\xi})\). Choose \(\xi>0\) sufficiently small. After each of the first two stages the number of unmatched targets is at most \(K_1=O(U^{1-3c})\), respectively \(K_2=O(U^{.76})\). At \(H_3\), the scaled intervals all exceed \(V^{17/30+.005}\), so the asserted tolerance holds in every cell. There is sufficient supply for every remaining target.

Within a cell and for each target sign, match remaining targets in increasing order to remaining opposite-sign partners in increasing order until one list is exhausted. Keep all earlier matches. A previous match within a cell consumes one from both corresponding lists, preserving their surplus. Take \(S\) to be precisely the endpoints of the matches.

If the GCD test is temporarily omitted, the signed ordered off-diagonal incidence in a node is \(s_J^2+2s_Jr_J-|J\cap S|\), with \(s_J,r_J\) the selected and surviving sign sums. The diagonal costs \(O(1)\) per shell. At \(H\le H_1\) the total absolute pair weight costs \(O(H_1^2/H_0)=O(1)\). At \(H_i\le H<H_{i+1}\) at most \(2K_i\) units of selected charge remain unpaired across the nodes in total. Using \(|s_J|,|r_J|\le H\) bounds the quadratic contributions by \(O(K_iH_{i+1}^2/(UH_0))\) after geometric summation. The exponents are \(-3c+2\eta+o(1)\) and \(.76+.16-1+o(1)\). Both are negative. At \(H\ge H_3\) every selected sign cancels within its node. These bounds also hold for any restriction on which full depths are summed.

## Restoring the original GCD test

For selected \(n=mp\), \(m\le X^\alpha<D\) and \(p\ge U/X^\alpha>D\). Therefore a distinct row \(r\) fails the test against \(n\) exactly when \(p\mid r\). This includes all interactions with unselected vertices. Within a node of length \(H\), at most \(2H/p\) distinct multiples of \(p\) can accompany \(n\); this bound also applies when \(H<p\) because then the count is zero. Multiply by the node weight and sum geometrically over lengths. The absolute weight touching \(n\) that can be rejected is \(O(H_0/(Up))\) for one orientation. Sum over at most \(U\) selected vertices and allow both orientations. Using \(p\ge U/X^\alpha\) gives \(O(2^{-s}X^\alpha)\) for the shell. This proves (1) for the masked signed difference, while keeping exactly the same graph on the survivors.

## Consequence for Step 4

The rough-source paper gives \(1+[C(\mathrm{all})]_+=X^{2b+o(1)}\), \(b=\sup_\rho\Re\rho-1/2\), on this same full graph or its coarse-only restriction. If \(b>0\), choose \(\alpha<2b\) before taking horizons. The survivors then have \(C(R)=X^{2b+o(1)}\) on all sufficiently late aligned cutoffs. Alternatively for each prescribed \(\epsilon>0\), select \(\alpha<\epsilon/2\) and the deterministic complement above. An independently proved \(C(R)\ll_\epsilon X^{\epsilon/2}\) on arbitrarily late aligned \(X\) for every \(\epsilon\) would contradict every \(b>0\). Such an upper is missing.

Each survivor has every prime factor greater than \(y\) and \(n/P^+(n)>X^\kappa\); the exact survivor graph also excludes the partners actually spent. Losing a positive fraction of vertices does not imply an exponent gain for its signed covariance. The prime–prime contribution by itself grows as \(X^{1-o(1)}\) with the native tree weights, by counting primes within the base cells and subtracting their diagonals. The signed incidence calculation pays that contribution only with the compensating edges retained in (1). There was no new Lean or production qualification of this deduction.
