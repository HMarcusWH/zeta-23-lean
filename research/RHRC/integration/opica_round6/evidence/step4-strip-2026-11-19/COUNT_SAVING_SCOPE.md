# Which objects the \(1/120000\) count saving controls

**Source audit, 19 November 2026.** The saving counts exceptional Hecke detector rows. It supplies neither a bound on the number of fourth powers violating the ordinary-prime lower bound nor a short-block sign theorem for the integrated ordinary-prime discrepancy.

## The original index

The 4 November repair, equation (2.7), gives the paper bound \(\#\mathcal B\le X^{R_\kappa(\delta,x)-1/120000}\). Here \(\mathcal B\) is a simultaneous-large-value fiber of the canonical Hecke detector over the Eisenstein integers. The rows carry a common character presentation, coprimality restrictions, prescribed detector profiles, marked prime-ideal slots and a height restriction. The exponent saving is relative to the old crossing bound \(R_\kappa\), approximately \(2/3\). It applies in the box \(0.38858\le\delta=2a-1\le0.38859\), \(0.49999\le x\le1/2\), with cutoff within \(10^{-4}\) of the balancing cutoff before rebalancing. The detector variable \(m\) is a normalized polynomial-length exponent (approximately \(0.408\) at the crossing). The physical integer indexing \(m^4\) has a different definition. Original detector and marked fourth moment, §7 · Coverage repair, §§2–4

The retained characters omit the fixed principal inducing row. In the corresponding elementary character calculation, adding a common value to every reduced residue-class response leaves all nonprincipal Fourier coefficients fixed while changing their principal sum. The later prime-only construction does recover the exact rational-prime channel from weighted prime ideals. Its principal row is a singleton. Four small divisor masks retain the cross-prime contribution, each as a multiple of the full response. This representation supplies no estimate for the principal sum at the successive cutoffs. Prime-only carrier and complete mask Gram, §§2–6

For \(M\le n\le 2M\), the relevant exceptional sets are \(\{n:C(n^4)<-n^2\log n/(2\pi)\}\) and \(\{n:A(n^4)>0\}\), where \(C=\pi-\operatorname{li}\) and \(A(x)=\int_2^x C(u)\,du\). The documents supply no implication from membership in either set to a qualifying detector row. Even a hypothetical injection into one fiber at \(X=M^4\) would give an upper of order \(M^{4(R_\kappa-1/120000)}\), an exponent exceeding 2, whereas there are only \(M+1\) endpoints. A different choice of \(X\) would require a source-preserving correspondence, its multiplicity bound, and coverage of all relevant detector classes. The two endpoint events also differ in sign and history. A deep negative instantaneous deficit can decrease \(A\) even when the earlier cumulative contribution is positive.

## The count that would suffice

**DERIVED on paper.** For any fixed \(0<\eta<1\), a genuine estimate \(\#\{M\le n\le 2M:A(n^4)>0\}=O(M^{1-\eta})\) for every large dyadic \(M\) would imply RH. The weaker requirement that the longest run of consecutive positive endpoints in each such dyad has length \(O(M^{1-\eta})\) also suffices.

Indeed, if the zero supremum is \(B>1/2\), fix \(0<\epsilon<\min(B-1/2,\eta/8)\). Johnston's integrated oscillation and the ordinary explicit formula give arbitrarily large \(x\) with \(A(x)\gg_\epsilon x^{1+B-\epsilon}\), and \(|C(u)|\ll_\epsilon x^{B+\epsilon}\) for \(x\le u\le2x\). Integrating this derivative bound shows \(A>0\) throughout \([x,x+c_\epsilon x^{1-2\epsilon}]\) with fixed sufficiently small \(c_\epsilon>0\). Consecutive fourth powers in this interval are spaced by \(O(x^{3/4})\). It therefore contains \(\gg x^{1/4-2\epsilon}\) consecutive positive endpoints. At least half the run lies in one dyad with \(M\asymp x^{1/4}\), giving \(\gg M^{1-8\epsilon}\) and contradicting the hypothesized bound. The dyadic count, the short-block sign condition, and an independent source estimate that implies either remain **OPEN**.
