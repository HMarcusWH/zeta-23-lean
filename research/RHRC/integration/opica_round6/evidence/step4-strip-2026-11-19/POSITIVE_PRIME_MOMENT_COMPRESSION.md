# A polynomial-size description of the ordinary-prime source with controlled error

**DERIVED on paper.** Write \(P_1(t)=\int j(t-u)\,d\nu(u)\) for the genuine ordinary-prime part of the OPICA field, with \(\nu=\sum_p(\log p)p^{-1/2}\delta_{\log p}\). Through \(u=T\), at most \(O_j((1+T)^3)\) weighted, actual ordinary primes suffice to represent \(P_1\) with pointwise error \(O_j(e^{-3t})\) on the positive half-line. The representing measure is fixed before choosing an observation or horizon. Its positive weights must be calculated from the true prime moments in the indicated cells. The bound counts the required data and supplies no upper on their coherent signed action.

Use the native pulse's verified support \([-s,s]\) and derivative budget \(\|j^{(L)}\|_\infty\le C A^L(L!)^2\). On each integer log shell \([m,m+1)\), put \(L=m+1\) and divide it into \(K=\lceil e^4 A L\rceil\) equal half-open cells. In each nonempty cell replace the restricted positive measure by at most \(L\) atoms on that cell's **actual** prime logs with the identical moments of degrees \(0,\dots,L-1\). Indeed the normalized moment vector lies in the convex hull of the finite vectors \((1,u,\ldots,u^{L-1})\); their affine dimension is at most \(L-1\). Removing one atom at a time along an affine dependence proves the positive representation directly. A lexicographic choice among the finitely many supports followed by solving the independent moment system fixes a single measure. Cells and shells do not depend on \(t\).

Taylor expansion of \(j(t-u)\) about a cell endpoint leaves absolute error at most
\[
 2\,\nu(\mathrm{cell})\, C(A/K)^L L!
 \ \le\ 2C\nu(\mathrm{cell})e^{-4L}.
\]
The total prime mass on \([m,m+1)\) is \(O((1+m)e^{m/2})\) even by integer counting. Because both measures are supported inside the original cells, only the bounded number of shells meeting \([t-s,t+s]\) can contribute to the actual field difference. Summing the bound gives \(O_j(e^{-3t})\). Each finite initial shell is retained with its recorded moments. For each fixed derivative order \(b\), the derivative budget replaces \(L!\) in the error by at most \(C_{j,b} L!(1+L)^{2b}\). Therefore each fixed derivative of the error also satisfies \(O_{j,b}(e^{-3t})\). Taking \(K=\lceil e^4 A L\log(m+3)\rceil\) instead gives an error decaying faster than every fixed exponential for \(O_j((1+T)^3\log(T+3))\) retained prime nodes.

For the localized Laguerre projections the exponentially small source difference is in \(L^2(0,\infty)\); each projection therefore differs by \(o(1)\), using unit norm, weak convergence and the unchanged modulation and taper. For native terminal windows one may use the pointwise error directly. The original higher prime powers are accounted for separately by their actual constant \(1/32\) and \(L^2\) remainder. This calculation does not change either that constant or the squarefree-divisor graph's individual edges. The comparison is at the level of the complete signed field. There is a less costly version when only poles or exponential rates in the projection order matter. Retain one full unit cell per shell, still matching degrees \(0,\dots,m\). For every disk \(|z|\le B\), Taylor expansion of \(e^{-zu}\) about \(m\) bounds the Laplace-transform error of a shell by
\[
 2C(1+m)e^{(B+1/2)m+B}\,B^{m+1}/(m+1)! .
\]
The series is locally uniformly convergent on the entire plane. Thus \(O((1+T)^2)\) representatives suffice to preserve each Laplace pole and its principal part wherever the original transform continues meromorphically. Multiplying by the entire native transform preserves that conclusion. After the Cayley substitution the discrepancy is holomorphic in each closed disk \(|w|\le r<1\), uniformly for modulation in a compact real interval. Cauchy's bound for each fixed \(r\) makes the error subexponential in the coefficient order. This version alone gives no bounded physical-time error.

The centered moment data consist of the differences between the true moments and those of \(e^{u/2}\,du\) on the same cells for large positive \(u\). The full continuum convolution vanishes at \(t>\log 2+s\) by \(J(1/2)=0\). A positive measure can have polynomially many representative atoms while producing exponentially growing signed projections: the previously tested modified Euler product has positive logarithmic-derivative weights, obeys the same mass bound, and admits the same compression while its specified rightward pole survives the entire discrepancy.
