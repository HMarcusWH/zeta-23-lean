### 11.1 Reuse M50 and audit the native ambient-commutator cancellation

M50 already gives `Q=-arch-prime` on both actual carriers. Preserve its canonical scalar and prime-power conventions; do not rebuild the theorem.

The previous review proposes the following **DERIVED native specialization**, to prove rather than assume. Let `E_0=-E_arch-E_prime`, `D_0=diag(n^2)` in actual centered Fourier indices. The production off-diagonal divided differences have the form

\[
(E_0)_{nm}=\frac{f_n-f_m}{n-m}\quad(n\ne m)
\]

for the actual combined real primitive f. With `g_n=n f_n`,

\[
[D_0,E_0]=g\mathbf1^*+f\mathbf n^*-\mathbf n f^*-\mathbf1g^*.
\]

The diagonal commutes with D0. Both boundary moment rows annihilate the carrier: `1*V=0` and `n*V=0`. The pole annihilation and self-adjointness kill the compressed pole commutator. This should yield

\[
V^*[D_0,E]V=0
\tag{NC}
\]

on the actual carrier. Search existing displacement declarations first and prove the exact centered-index, complex-conjugation, source and carrier adapters. If a source convention introduces an extra term, retain it and repair the target explicitly; do not delete it to obtain NC.

equivalently `tr(G^-1 F G^-1 F)` in a complete nonorthogonal frame. Hermitian spectral control then gives a polynomial lower bound. TR2 also controls positive eigenvalues and may be unnecessarily strong: a direct one-sided form estimate is fully acceptable.

### 11.4 Resurrected alternative: one-sided nonbacktracking transfer

The coarse-growth paper defines the actual self-adjoint prime-power operators on the physical moment carrier,

\[
B_n=-\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,P_p(S_{\log n}+S_{\log n}^*)P_p.
\]

Its unconditional paper bounds are `||B_n||<=2` and `sum_n ||B_n v||^2<=4 L^3 ||v||^2`. These are not new Lean results until the native translation/source adapter and inequalities are compiled. Their polynomial cost is compatible with PG even though it does not approach zero. [S5]

For `(N_L z)_i=sum_(j!=i) B_j z_j`, the paper's one-sided transfer uses a bound on real negative eigenvalues only. If individual norms are at most a, the square-function constant is b and no real eigenvalue lies below -R, its conclusion is `sum_i B_i >= -(a+R+2b)I`. The still-unproved native input is

\[
\operatorname{spec}(N_{p,L})\cap\mathbb R
\subseteq[-C(1+L)^d,\infty)
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }L
\tag{NB}
\]

in one fixed parity. The Archimedean lower constant and all source adapters remain part of the final composition.

Do not replace NB by coefficient-size statistics: aligned scalar operators satisfy small individual/square-function costs while having an exponentially negative nonbacktracking eigenvalue. A candidate must exploit the actual translations, projection and arithmetic.

Retain ordered products `P T_i P T_j P`; replacing them by `P T_i T_j P` loses `P T_i(I-P)T_jP`. Zero-extended opposite translations are not unrestricted group inverses. Equal-product regrouping must preserve exit boundaries and intermediate projections.

A sufficient full spectral-radius moment estimate is optional, not required. If used, retain the full dimension factor in `rho(N)^(2k)<=||N^k||_F^2`; the paper proposes k growing with L to pay an exponentially growing dimension. N is generally nonnormal: a radius estimate is not an operator-norm estimate. No such native word bound is currently proved.
