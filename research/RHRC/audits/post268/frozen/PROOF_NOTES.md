## 1. Strict parity separation forces a simple constrained ground state

### 1.1 Objects and hypotheses

Fix **L > 0** and **K >= 2**. Let E and O be the exact Euclidean even and odd boundary-flat spaces at size K. Let A and B be the corresponding self-adjoint compressed canonical operators. Let

- D : E -> O be the existing algebraic linear equivalence;
- g in O be the odd cubic compression vector;
- S : E -> C be the canonical quadratic source-moment linear functional.

The source provides the rank-one identity

\[
 BD-DA=gS.
\]

Here `(gS)(v)=S(v)g`. D is **not assumed unitary or isometric**. Neither A nor B is assumed positive. These are compressed operators: an eigenvector of a compression is not automatically an eigenvector of the uncompressed ambient matrix.

The two direct source statements used are:

`GlobalParityBottomIntertwining.lean::evenEigenmode_shiftedOdd_eq_sourceCubic`

and

`GlobalParityBottomIntertwining.lean::oddEigenmode_pulledBack_shiftedEven_eq_neg_sourceCubic`.

They explicitly require positive L and legal K. `GlobalParityBottomSpectrum.lean` supplies attainment of both parity bottoms at successor sizes. The exact quadratic/norm splitting identifies the complete boundary-flat compression with the orthogonal direct sum of A and B.

### 1.2 Generic rank-one lemma

**Lemma — DERIVED.** If an eigenvalue a of A is not in the spectrum of B, then its eigenspace has complex dimension at most one.

**Proof.** For any v in ker(A-aI),

\[
 (B-aI)Dv=S(v)g.
\]

Since B-aI is invertible,

\[
 v=S(v)D^{-1}(B-aI)^{-1}g.
\]

The right side lies in one fixed complex line. Thus the entire eigenspace has dimension at most one. Since a is an eigenvalue, the eigenspace is nonzero, so its dimension is exactly one. This argument uses invertibility of D, not preservation of its metric. QED.

The symmetric version is equally important. For w in ker(B-bI), set v=D^{-1}w. The reverse identity gives

\[
 (A-bI)v=-S(v)D^{-1}g.
\]

If b is not in the spectrum of A, the pulled-back eigenspace lies in the line generated by `(A-bI)^{-1}D^{-1}g`, and the original B-eigenspace has dimension one. QED.

Equivalently, a nonzero eigenspace on either strict branch admits an injective linear map into C via the relevant source functional. This is also consistent with the already proved nonvanishing of the fourth moment on every nonzero strict-even ground eigenvector.

### 1.3 Ground-state consequence

Write a = lambda_min(A), b = lambda_min(B).

If a < b, then B-aI is positive definite and invertible. The preceding lemma proves that the A-ground eigenspace has dimension one. Since b>a, the complete boundary-flat ground eigenspace is exactly that A-ground eigenspace.

If b < a, the reverse argument proves the analogous statement for B.

If a=b, bottom attainment supplies a nonzero ground vector in each of the orthogonal parity spaces. Their direct sum gives at least two independent ground vectors of the complete boundary-flat compression.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{the complete boundary-flat ground eigenspace has dimension one}
\iff \lambda_{\rm even}\ne\lambda_{\rm odd}.}
\]

This is an ordinary consequence of the existing theorem stack, not a new Lean theorem. In a self-adjoint finite-dimensional setting geometric and algebraic multiplicities agree, so 'simple' is justified.

### 1.4 What it buys and what it does not

It localizes **ground-state multiplicity** to parity ties. On either strict branch, a normalized source-functional chart can select the ground line. This is useful for the unconditional ground-weight atlas and for a later local perturbation theorem.

A claim about differentiability or real-analyticity in L still requires the corresponding regularity of the operator family and a perturbation argument. Simplicity by itself is not a proved derivative formula. At a tie, work with the complete ground subspace rather than a numerically selected vector.

The result does not show that one parity is always lower, that either bottom is nonnegative, that parity ties are impossible, or that the original CCM **full-space** even-simple hypothesis holds. The full ambient carrier and boundary-flat carrier differ. The latter has zero boundary evaluation and cannot be substituted for a vector requiring a nonzero boundary normalization in a different construction.

No claim of novelty relative to all mathematical literature is made.

## 2. Exact counterexample to carrier-wide positivity of the prime-test weight

### 2.1 A legal even boundary-flat vector

Use K=4, with centered indices n=-4,...,4, and

\[
 u=(-1,3,1,-15,24,-15,1,3,-1).
\]

It is real and even. Direct integer arithmetic gives

\[
 M_0=M_1=M_2=M_3=0,\qquad M_4=-24,
 \qquad M_k=\sum_{n=-4}^{4}u_n n^k.
\]

In particular it belongs to the exact boundary-flat carrier.

Let the source atom matrix be

\[
 S_{nn}(w)=2w\cos(2\pi nw),
\]

\[
 S_{nm}(w)=\frac{\sin(2\pi nw)-\sin(2\pi mw)}{\pi(n-m)},\quad n\ne m,
\]

and set `g(w)=u^T S(w)u`. This is the real specialization of the repository's source-atom energy.

Its derivative entries are

\[
 S'_{nn}(w)=2\cos(2\pi nw)-4\pi nw\sin(2\pi nw),
\]

\[
 S'_{nm}(w)=2\frac{n\cos(2\pi nw)-m\cos(2\pi mw)}{n-m}.
\]

Substitution at w=1/8, using exact trigonometric values, gives

\[
\boxed{g'(1/8)=\frac{25432}{21}-\frac{15962}{35}\sqrt2
                 -126\sqrt2\,\pi-2\pi<0.}
\]

The decimal value, **only as a diagnostic**, is approximately

`-0.00101311321020509538025577616452789276340903438`.

### 2.2 Rational sign certificate

Set

\[
 r=\frac{1414213562373095}{10^{15}},\qquad
 p=\frac{3141592653589793}{10^{15}}.
\]

Integer squaring proves `r^2<2`, and r>0. Thus r<sqrt(2).

For p<pi use Machin's identity

\[
 \pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239).
\]

For completeness, tan(2 arctan(1/5))=5/12 and tan(4 arctan(1/5))=120/119. The tangent subtraction formula gives tan(4 arctan(1/5)-arctan(1/239))=1; its angle is in (0,pi/2), proving the identity. Alternating arctangent-series bounds, with 16 terms at 1/5 and 9 terms at 1/239, give a rational lower bound for pi which is strictly larger than p. All comparisons are integer/rational operations in the supplied checker.

The expression for g'(1/8) decreases as either positive variable sqrt(2) or pi increases. Therefore it is strictly less than

\[
 \frac{25432}{21}-\frac{15962}{35}r-126rp-2p
 =-\frac{2127537741253160308202196041}
          {2100000000000000000000000000000}<0.
\]

This does not depend on a floating-point sign decision.

### 2.3 It also has the positive endpoint jet

An independent exact Taylor calculation gives

\[
 g^{(1)}(0)=g^{(3)}(0)=g^{(5)}(0)=g^{(7)}(0)=0,
 \quad g^{(9)}(0)=1152(2\pi)^8>0.
\]

The function g is odd, so its even derivatives vanish at zero as well. For each L>0 define the corresponding weight

\[
 w_{L,u}(t)=g'(1-t/L).
\]

Then

\[
 w_{L,u}^{(j)}(L)=0\ (0\le j\le7),\qquad
 w_{L,u}^{(8)}(L)=1152(2\pi)^8/L^8>0,
\]

but

\[
 w_{L,u}(7L/8)=g'(1/8)<0.
\]

**Conclusion — DERIVED, with executed symbolic/rational checks:** evenness, boundary-flatness and the positive leading endpoint jet do not imply pointwise nonnegativity of the prime-test weight.

This vector has **not** been shown to be a canonical ground eigenvector. The counterexample does not disprove a more restrictive ground-eigenvector-specific law, canonical energy positivity, or RH. It blocks an overgeneralization, not the desired final theorem.

## 3. Exact conditional closure by cofinal spectral lower certificates

### 3.1 Source facts used

For N>=1 and L>0 define

\[
 \lambda_N(L)=\texttt{globalParitySuccessorBottom}\ L\ N.
\]

The parameter N is the predecessor index: the actual parity matrix size parameter is N+1.

4. The terminal zeta-configuration/Mathlib-RH seam and the canonical positivity equivalence are already established in their audited modules.

### 3.2 Proposed certificate hypothesis — OPEN

Find a specified sequence of positive apertures `L_j -> infinity`. For every j, construct an unbounded sequence of legal indices `N_{j,m}` and explicit nonnegative errors `epsilon_{j,m} -> 0` such that

\[
\tag{C}
 \lambda_{N_{j,m}}(L_j)\ge -\epsilon_{j,m}
 \quad\text{for every }m.
\]

The estimates must hold for the exact canonical matrices, uniformly over all vectors in the relevant carrier. Finite numerical samples do not establish (C). The certificate can use every sufficiently large N as a special case, but an unbounded certified subsequence suffices.

### 3.3 Proof that (C) closes the route

Fix j and any legal N0. Suppose lambda_N0(L_j)=-delta<0. Choose m so large that N_{j,m}>=N0 and epsilon_{j,m}<delta. Antitonicity gives

\[
 \lambda_{N_{j,m}}(L_j)\le-\delta,
\]

whereas (C) gives

\[
 \lambda_{N_{j,m}}(L_j)\ge-\epsilon_{j,m}>-\delta.
\]

Contradiction. Therefore every legal finite bottom at each L_j is nonnegative.

If RH failed, the terminal seam supplies an off-line zeta zero. Aperture freedom supplies L0 such that every L>L0 has a negative finite witness. Choose j with L_j>L0. That witness contradicts the preceding nonnegativity conclusion. Hence RH.

This is a complete ordinary proof of the **conditional implication `(C) -> RH`**, not a proof of (C). Under the already proved canonical positivity equivalence, RH implies (C) by taking epsilon=0. Thus this is another RH-strength criterion; it is not advertised as a weaker theorem or a solved estimate.

Do not substitute an archimedean quadrature cutoff T for the Fourier-size parameter N. A small numerical residual is not a lower spectral certificate unless its operator-norm error is enclosed. For a first-contact alternative, a nonnegative first derivative at a zero is not sufficient: `f(t)=-t^3` crosses down while f'(0)=0. A valid barrier must control the first nonzero contact order or a stronger local invariance condition. At parity ties, all ground directions matter.
